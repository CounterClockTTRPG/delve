The host and co-hosts run the table. The buttons for that are at the head of the play screen, and **The host's
controls** are in the side column.

## Start

While the table gathers, players can talk and ask the DM, but not act. When everyone who's coming has arrived:

1. Choose **Start the session**.
2. For each character whose player isn't here, choose whether the DM plays them until their player arrives, they stay
   in the background, or they sit this session out.
3. Choose **Start the session** again. The DM opens the scene; latecomers join in as they come.

At a table with only you, your first act starts the session.


## Beats

Each player writes an act, or passes. The DM answers once everyone present has acted, or when you choose **Go**.
Under **The host's controls**, a **Beat timer** closes the beat by itself, and you choose what happens then to
characters whose player hasn't acted: they hold back, or the DM plays them cautiously.

You see what the other seats don't: the DM's tools, folded into one line each, and any note the DM writes for the
host alone.

## When someone steps away

A player can **Step away** for a while, and beats stop waiting for them. You can do it for them too: under **The
host's controls**, **Mark away** on their seat (and **Back** when they return). A player who acts is back by
themselves.

Below the seats, **While a player is away** sets each character's part for the rest of the session: **Played by
their player**, **Are played cautiously by the DM**, **Stay in the background** or **Sit the rest of the session
out**. Your choice wins over the player's own. A player who just doesn't act in time is covered by the beat timer's
fallback instead.

## Table talk and raised hands

**Table talk** is out of character, for the people at the table; it never goes to the DM as an act. A player who
wants a word before anyone acts chooses **Raise hand**, with their point if they like. Raised hands show at the top
of the side column, and as a line by the act box. **Lower** one when it is dealt with, or **Take a break** from there
to talk it through.

## Writing to the table

With mail set up on this Delve, **Write to the table** sits in the side column. Give a **Subject** and a **Message**,
and send it to **Everyone at the table** or **Only the ones I tick**; observers aren't among them. Delve sends one email
to each, and no one sees anyone's address. Only those with a confirmed address who haven't switched these emails off get
it: when you write to everyone, the line under the message says how many. Your words go as you wrote them, not
translated.

Replies to the email aren't read, unless you tick **Let them reply to my address**: then a reply goes to your own
address, which you add and confirm first under **Email** in **Preferences**. A table sends at most ten messages a day.

## Breaks and the end

**Take a break** stops the story where it is; **Back to the story** goes on. To finish, choose **End the session**:
the DM brings the scene to a pause and writes the recap, which opens the next session. If a beat is open, press
**Go** first.

## How the DM plays

**Table options**, at the head of the screen, set the **Tone** (or a **Custom** one in your own words), the
**Detail** and the DM's **Effort**. On the campaign's page, **At the table** turns **Voice** on or off and sets
**Initiative** to **Per combatant** or **Per side**.

To see a whole map with its secrets, open the campaign's page, **Maps**, then **DM view**. It shows everything, so
close it before you play.
