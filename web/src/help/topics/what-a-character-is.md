Your character is a person in the story, with a sheet that says what they can do.

- **Abilities** are six numbers from 3 to 18: **Strength**, **Dexterity**, **Constitution**, **Intelligence**,
  **Wisdom** and **Charisma**. Higher is better. A strong character hits harder; a wise one notices more.
- **Class** is their calling. A **fighter** fights; a **mage** casts spells but is frail; a **cleric** heals and
  serves a god; a **thief** sneaks, picks locks and finds traps.
- **Race** is their people: human, elf, dwarf, halfling, gnome or half-elf. Each has its own gifts, such as an elf's
  sharp eyes or a dwarf's sense for stonework.
- **Hit points** are how much harm they can take. At 0, they fall.
- **Armour class** is how hard they are to hit. In these games, a *lower* armour class is better.
- **Level** shows how experienced they are. Characters earn **experience points** by surviving, winning and finding
  treasure, and go up a level as they learn.

## Who they are

The sheet says what your character *can* do. Who they *are* is up to you: brave or careful, kind or greedy, chatty or
quiet. You don't need to act it out with voices; "Mira doesn't trust him and keeps her hand on her dagger" is
roleplaying too.
