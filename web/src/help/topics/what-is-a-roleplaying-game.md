A roleplaying game is a story told by a group. Each player takes one person in the story, their **character**, and
says what that character does. One more person, the **Dungeon Master** (the DM), describes the world and plays
everyone else in it. In Delve, Claude is the DM.

Nobody wins and nobody loses. There's no board and no script. The story goes where the players take it. The DM says
what the world does in return, and the rules and the dice decide what is left to chance.

## A conversation

It is a conversation more than a game of turns:

- **The DM** sets the scene: "The tunnel opens on a cave. Water drips somewhere in the dark, and something has
  scratched marks into the wall."
- **You** say what your character does: "I hold my torch up to the marks and look at them."
- **The DM** tells you what happens next, and the story moves on.

You don't need to know the rules to start. The DM knows them and applies them for you.
