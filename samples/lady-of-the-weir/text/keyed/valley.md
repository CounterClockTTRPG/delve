# The Ambresleigh valley

The valley is small: an hour's walk end to end, a quarter-hour between neighbouring places. Check for a wandering
encounter (1 in 6, on the table of the ground the party crosses) whenever the party walks between two places, and
once at dusk and once at midnight. After dark the river path between the weir and the ford uses
[the night table](encounter:river-path-by-night).

The trouble has a clock: [[secret:the-three-nights]].

## V1. Ambresleigh {#v1}

> A dozen cottages of wattle and limewash round a muddy green, their roofs thatched with reed. Long racks of eels hang
> to smoke over slow fires by the water. People talk quietly here, and every door has a twist of river-weed nailed
> over it.

Ambresleigh has about sixty souls. They catch eels in wicker traps, smoke them and sell them in Hask, and they have
paid the Lady of the Weir a dish of milk and a silver penny each new moon for as long as anyone remembers. Since the
pool went black they have caught nothing but dead eels, and they keep their doors barred after dusk.

The villagers tell what they know freely if anyone stands them a drink or helps with the work; roll on
[the rumours](text:valley-key#rumours) or pick. Everyone blames [[npc:odo-tesk]]. Nobody wants to talk about the
Reeve's daughter.

The weed over the doors is a charm against the drowned dead. It works about as well as such charms do.

## V2. The Reeve's Hall {#v2}

> The only house in Ambresleigh with a stone footing and an upper floor. Bundles of notched tally-sticks hang over the
> door, the village's eel-tithe for ten years.

[[npc:reeve-haldan]] lives here with his daughter [[npc:wenna]]. He meets the party at the door, pays them 10 gp of
the promised 50 on account, puts them up in the loft, and talks a great deal about Odo Tesk's pumps.

**The three lost**, as Haldan tells it, on three nights running, the first five nights ago, all on the river path
between the weir and the ford after dusk; nobody since, and the village holds its breath: **Coll**, the miller's son (Magge's boy), going to his night-traps; **Annet**, the tinker's
wife, walking home from the ford; and **old Brisk** the drover, who took the river path because it was shorter.
Brisk's lantern was found on the path, still upright, burnt out. (All three are in the Sluice-House now, as Ostric's
zombies: [S5](text:sluice-key#s5).)

**Clues.** A mason's maul leans by the hearth, its head white with stone dust; Haldan says he has been mending the
byre (the byre is not mended). Wenna's window looks out on the weir, and its sill is strewn with river-glass beads
she has not strung.

**Wenna** is quiet and watchful. Alone with someone kind she admits she has heard a woman singing in the reeds at
dusk, singing her name, and that she isn't afraid of it. She doesn't know what her father did.

## V3. The Mill {#v3}

> A timber mill on the race, its wheel turning slowly in the thin water Odo's pumps have left it. Flour dust and the
> smell of wet wood.

[[npc:magge]] the miller runs it alone since her son Coll went missing on the river path five nights ago. She is
Wenna's aunt, short-tempered and frightened, and she knows about the Lady's choosing: her own mother told her of
Sorrel, fifty years since. She won't say a word of it until the party has done something for the village (found
Dilly, killed something that walks at night, or brought her news of Coll); then she tells all of
[[secret:the-consort]], and that Haldan "went out with his maul the night of the new moon and came home white as
milk."

## V4. The Weir {#v4}

> A shelf of turf above the weir, worn bare by feet. A low stone with a dish hollowed in its top stands by the water,
> cracked clean through. Rags and river-glass beads hang in the willows. Below the weir the pool lies flat and black,
> and nothing moves in it.

**The offering-stone** has been split with a hammer: a thief or a dwarf sees the blows at once, and fresh chips
lie in the grass. While it is broken the Lady is weak, the pool is foul, and the dead of the Sluice-House walk.

**The shrine.** The willows hung with rags and river-glass, and the broken stone among them, are the Lady's shrine:
there is no building, only the trees and the offerings of a hundred years. This is what [[npc:brother-aldo]] means to
burn, at dawn on the party's second day ([[quest:the-friars-torch]]).

**The heron.** A grey heron stands on the weir whenever the party comes, and watches. It is the Lady's eye. If anyone
feeds it, speaks to it kindly or leaves an offering on the broken stone, the Lady remembers (see below). If anyone
harms it, she remembers that too.

**The Lady at dusk.** At dusk, if someone leaves an offering (a coin, milk, a song, something dear), mist rises off
the pool and [[npc:the-lady]] speaks from the reeds: a woman's voice, amused and cold. She will bargain. What she
wants and what she will take is set out under [the endings](text:valley-key#endings).

**What she tells, and when.** She knows who broke her stone, but she is proud and doesn't carry tales: she answers in
hints, and the party should have to work for the rest. At a first visit she says only that "one who loves what I have
chosen" broke it, that "something old walks out of the arch since my water went down", and that she will bargain
with whoever brings her better than copper. She never tells Ostric's story: that is the inscription's to tell
([S4](text:sluice-key#s4)). She names Haldan only when the party already knows (they have found the maul, heard Magge,
or Haldan has confessed) or brings her a real offering at a second dusk, and then she names him with contempt.

**The pumps.** Two big leather-and-timber pumps stand on the south bank, worked by Odo's men in the day. They have
lowered the pool by a yard and laid bare the arch of the Sluice-House ([V5](text:valley-key#v5)). The water they draw
is black and smells of old iron.

The top of the weir is a yard of wet stone: anyone crossing it in a hurry or in a fight makes a Dexterity check or
falls into the pool (no harm but a soaking, a lost torch and the cold).

## V5. The Sluice-House Arch {#v5}

> Below the weir's south end a stone arch, half buried in the bank, gapes over a dry channel of green-slimed stone. An
> iron grille closes it, and someone has hung a new padlock on the grille.

The way into [the Sluice-House](text:sluice-key#s1). Odo's padlock: [[npc:tam-ruddle]] has the key; a thief can pick
it; two strong characters can lever the grille off its rotten hinges in a turn (combined Strength 32+), with noise
enough to wake the place. Fresh boot-prints go in, and run out. Something dragged has left a long smear on the slime,
going in.

## V6. Weirholt {#v6}

> A squat stone house inside a wall, on the only rise for a mile. A Tesk pennant, green and silver, hangs limp over the
> gate.

[[npc:odo-tesk]]'s manor; see [Weirholt](text:weirholt-key#w1). By day his men are at the pumps; by night they are
inside with the gate barred, and nobody answers knocking after dark unless it is plainly the living.

## V7. The Reeds {#v7}

> Reed-beds taller than a man, cut by eel-catchers' channels. The ground gives underfoot and the frogs never stop.

Moving through the reeds off the channels halves speed. The eel-catchers' wicker traps lie everywhere, full of dead
eels. Use [the reeds table](encounter:reeds) here.

[[npc:dilly]], the eel-catcher's child, has been hiding here since yesterday's dusk, too frightened to go home. A
party that searches the reeds for a turn finds her up a willow; she has seen "the wet men" come out of the arch at
night carrying someone. Bringing her home unharmed is [[quest:dilly-home]].

## V8. The Ford {#v8}

> Where the old road wades the Ambre: a gravel bar, a post for the ferry-rope long gone, a cold fire-ring.

**By day,** [[npc:merrit-vane]], a boatman of the Ferrymen's Guild of Hask, sits here with a rod he never baits,
asking eel-catchers how the Lady fares. He is the second suspect the village has: see [[rumor:ferrymen]].

**By night,** smugglers from Hask's thieves' guild cross here with packhorses, four or five of them
([the road table's](encounter:road) bandits), heading for the old watchtower ([V9](text:valley-key#v9)). They fight
only if cornered and would rather pay a toll in brandy.

## V9. The Old Watchtower {#v9}

> A stump of Thaumarch stone on a knoll, roofless, its stair winding up to nothing.

The smugglers' cache, under a slab at the foot of the stair (a search finds it): two bolts of southern silk (30 gp
each), a cask of good brandy (30 gp, heavy) and a purse of 80 sp. Taking it makes enemies in Hask, which is a story
for another day. From the top of the stair the whole valley lies open, and on a clear dusk you can see a pale shape
moving in the reeds by the pool.

## V10. The Mound {#v10}

> A long grassy mound with a single grey stone at its head. Cattle won't graze it, and the thorn trees round it lean
> away.

Sorrel lies here, the Lady's last consort, who walked out of the pool after twenty years of marriage and died on the
bank. At dusk or later his ghost, [[npc:sorrel]], sits on the stone, grey and dripping. He only talks: he is sad,
courteous and very tired. He can tell the party everything in [[secret:the-consort]], and that the Lady never forgave
him for leaving.

He asks one thing: his ring, a plain silver band with a heron cut on it, which he lost when he died. (Odo's men dug it
out of the bank; it is in Odo's strongbox, [W4](text:weirholt-key#w4).) Returning it to the stone lays him to rest:
[[quest:sorrel-at-rest]].

## The endings {#endings}

The trouble ends when the Lady is either restored or gone. The players choose; each way has a price.

**Restore the stone.** A new offering-stone (any stone with a dish cut in it, an afternoon's work for a mason or a
dwarf) set by the weir, with an offering, by the hand that broke the old one: Haldan's, which means the party must
bring him to it. Told he must give Wenna up, he refuses and bars his door; what moves him is not the party but
[[npc:magge]], once she knows her son is dead because of him (she dresses the new stone herself from an old
millstone), or Wenna, when she tells him she was never afraid. The Lady wakes in strength that same dusk. The pool clears, the Warden and his dead fall still where
they stand, and she takes Wenna as promised, to be her consort under the water. Wenna goes, more willing than her
father would like. This ends [[quest:the-ladys-matter]], but Wenna is not alive and free.

**Strike a new bargain.** At the pool at dusk the Lady will release Wenna for another price. She names it from what
the party has done: something dear to one of them, a secret no one else knows, a year's service from one character
(she calls when she needs a thing done on the river), or another mortal who will come to her willingly. She would
take Odo gladly; Odo is not willing. She mends her own stone in exchange (the new stone is still needed). She will
not take Haldan: "I do not marry fools." This ends the quest with Wenna free.

**Let the Lady fade.** Destroy the Warden and his dead by force, leave the stone broken, and refuse the bargain. The
Lady fades over a month to a voice, and then to nothing; the pool becomes ordinary water, clear again in a season.
Wenna is free. The village has lost its god, and the eel-catchers don't thank the party for it. Downriver at Hask, on
the next Ferry Night, the Ferryman takes his tithe with something like a smile ([[secret:ferryman-glad]]).

Whichever way it goes, the Lady keeps her word, and anyone who was kind to the heron hears her laugh once, in the
reeds, when they leave the valley.
