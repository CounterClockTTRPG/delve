# The Sluice-House

Nearly twelve hundred years ago, in the last days of the Thaumarchs, a sorcerer-prince's overseer named Ostric built a dam
across the Ambre to sell its water to the mills downstream, and worked his labourers to death building it. The river
had a god already. She drowned the works, and Ostric with his men in it, and pinned him under his own floor with a
blade of hers. The dam became her weir. While her stone stood and her blade stayed in the seal, he slept.

Since Odo's pumps drew the pool down, the Sluice-House is dry. Since Odo's men pulled the blade out, Ostric is awake:
**the Sluice-Warden**, a ghoul in the rags of his office, with his chained labourers as his skeletons and the three
people he has taken from the river path as his zombies. He is cunning and patient and hungry, and he wants out. Each
night he goes out a little further ([[secret:the-three-nights]]).

It is dark inside. Check for a wandering encounter on [the Sluice-House table](encounter:sluice-house) every second
turn the party spends here. Undead don't check morale.

## S1. The Intake {#s1}

> A dry stone channel, a step down from its walkways, floored with green slime and the bones of fish. An iron grille
> closes the way in, and someone has hung a new padlock on it.

The grille: [[npc:tam-ruddle]]'s key, a thief's pick-locks roll, or combined Strength of 32 or more to lever it off
its rotten hinges in a turn (noisy: roll a wandering encounter at once). The slime is slippery: running or fighting in
the channel itself needs a Dexterity check each round or the character falls (lose the round). The walkways either
side are dry. The long smear on the slime leads east.

## S2. The Valve Gallery {#s2}

> A vaulted hall full of rusted bronze: valve-wheels as tall as a man, rods running up into the roof, sluice-gates
> hanging in slots. The biggest wheel stands in the middle on a grating over the dark.

**The trap.** The great wheel still turns, stiffly. Anyone who turns it (and the Thaumarch lettering on its rim,
"OPEN THE RIVER", invites it) feels the grating under their feet give way: a 10-foot drop into the old wheel-pit,
1d6 damage, a Dexterity check to leap clear. A thief who searches the grating finds the release; a dwarf notices that
the grating hangs on the wheel's axle. Nothing else happens: the river is long gone from these pipes. Odo's sketch
([W4](text:weirholt-key#w4)) warns about the wheel.

**Machinery.** The bronze fittings are worth money in Hask (Odo's plan exactly): the smaller valve-wheels come off
with an hour's work, 40 gp of bronze each and 50 pounds in weight. There are six.

The iron door north to [S3](text:sluice-key#s3) is closed; something has scratched at it from the other side.

## S3. The Labourers' Hall {#s3}

> A long low barracks. Iron rings in the walls, chains, and men in rags standing against them, their heads bowed.

Six **skeletons** in rusted chains: Ostric's labourers. They don't move until someone living crosses the middle of
the room or touches the chains, then all six tear free and attack. A cleric of 1st level turns them on a 10 or more
(see [[monster:skeleton]]); a turned skeleton cowers against the wall in its chains and doesn't come back this
encounter.

The north-east corner has fallen in, and the passage beyond ends in clay after twenty feet. Under the fallen stones,
a labourer's tally-stick carved with forty-one notches and a heron: some of the labourers prayed to the Lady even
then.

## S4. The Inscription {#s4}

> A round chamber with a domed roof. Writing in a spiral covers the wall from floor to crown. A statue of a man in a
> long robe, a key at his belt, has had its face chiselled off.

The writing is in the old Thaumarch court script. A magic-user can read it with *read magic* or *comprehend
languages*, or with an Intelligence check and an hour's work (a sage or the Collegium at Hask would do it for 20 gp).
It is Ostric's boast in his own words, all the way up the dome; the slab at the bottom, cut deeper, is the Lady's
answer:

> *Here Ostric, Warden of the Sluice, who sold the river and drowned his men for it, lies under his own floor. He
> shall lie while my stone stands by my water and my blade stands in his seal. When the stone breaks and the blade is
> drawn, let them that broke and drew make it right, or answer to him.*

A party that reads it knows that both the broken stone and the drawn blade are to blame, and that it is the Lady's
stone that matters most.

## S5. The Seal Chamber {#s5}

> An eight-sided hall whose vault has sagged, its floor a great disc of bronze set in stone and written over with a
> ring of letters. The disc is cracked across, and a narrow slot gapes at its centre. It is cold here, and it smells
> of the river.

**The Sluice-Warden** ([[npc:ostric]]) waits here with three **zombies**: Coll the miller's son, Annet the tinker's
wife and old Brisk the drover, the vanished, still in their own clothes. A party that recognises Coll (Magge's son,
fair-haired, a mill-apron) should be told so; it is meant to hurt. The zombies shamble forward first; Ostric hangs
back and goes for anyone who is down, alone or carrying a light. His touch paralyses (save versus paralysis) like any
ghoul's. **He doesn't eat the paralysed:** he wants them as zombies for his march on the village, so he drags a
paralysed character towards the stone chest (a round to reach it, a round to start the binding), which gives the
party a round or two to save their friend. Only if he is cornered and hurt does he bite to kill. First-level
characters in OSRIC can have one or two hit points; this is the module's deadliest moment, and it should be
frightening rather than a quick death. A 1st-level cleric turns the zombies on 13 or more and Ostric on 16; he is the toughest thing in the valley.

**The blade.** *Eelbite* lies where Odo's men dropped it, against the south wall by an empty stone chest: a short
sword of grey-green bronze that doesn't tarnish, its hilt bound in eel-skin. It is a *short sword +1*. Thrust back
into the slot in the seal while Ostric stands on the disc, it pins him under his floor again for a night and a day,
long enough to run, or to mend the stone; it can't hold him for good while the Lady's stone is broken. Once the Lady
is restored she seals him herself and lets the party keep the blade: "Keep it, little thieves. It liked you."

**Treasure.** Ostric wears his chain of office, silver links and a bronze key, worth 150 gp. The stone chest held his
grave-goods: it is empty except for a gold ring the labourers missed (25 gp).

Destroying Ostric here ends his march on the village: what is left of his dead (the zombies, the skeletons of S3)
falls still within the day. It doesn't settle the Lady's matter by itself; that still needs one of
[the endings](text:valley-key#endings).

## S6. The Store {#s6}

> A low cave of a room hacked out of the clay behind the gallery, full of rotten crates and bronze.

Rats have nested here (2d4 **giant rats** that attack anything that disturbs the crates). Among the rubbish: a box of
Thaumarch tally-tokens, bronze, worth 20 gp to a collector; an overseer's ledger, rotted to a brick except for its
last page, which lists the labourers by number, forty-one of them, with "drowned" written beside every one.
