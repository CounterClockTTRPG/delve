# Aun: the world bible

The canon of Delve's own setting (DEL-351). Every page, fact and map follows this file; when a page needs a fact this
file doesn't give, the writer invents it within these lines and keeps it small and local. Owner's direction
(2026-11-01): the feel of early roleplaying and 1970s sword-and-sorcery (Leiber, Vance, Moorcock, Howard): wonder,
danger, roguish heroes. A loose likeness of Greyhawk's shape, original content. High magic belongs to the deep past and
returns only as the odd artifact dug up; ordinary people live far from magic; player characters are adventurers, not
heroes by default. The one new idea: gods great and small.

## Never

- No name, place, god, event or text from Greyhawk, the Forgotten Realms, any other published setting or novel. No
  Oerth, Flanaess, Circle of Eight, Iuz, Vecna, Suel, Lankhmar, Melniboné, Hyboria, Dying Earth names. The likeness is
  of shape and tone only.
- No high-fantasy register: no prophecies, chosen ones, noble elves saving the day, or good-versus-evil crusades taken
  at face value. No modern idiom ("okay", "deal with it", "vibe").
- No common magic: no magic shops, no village wizards, no healing potions for sale. A spellcaster is rare and watched.

## The world

**Aun** is the world; the known lands are **the Reaches**, a long continent about 1,600 miles west to east and 1,000
north to south. North is the cold **Iron Sea**; south, the warm **Sunder Sea**. Years are counted from the Unmaking:
it is **1188 RY** (Reckoned Year). A campaign starts on 1 Isaine 1188 RY, in early spring.

## The deep past

- **The Elder Days.** Elves and dwarves are older than human memory. Humans lived in tribes and worshipped the gods
  directly; the gods walked closer then.
- **The Thaumarchy** (about 1500 to 0 before the Unmaking, "BU"). An empire of sorcerer-princes, the **Thaumarchs**, of
  **Seven Houses**, ruling from **Ishavel, the Lantern of the World**, in the east-centre of the Reaches. Magic was in
  daily life: lit streets, towers raised past the clouds, roads that did not wear, servants of brass. The empire
  ruled every human land of the Reaches; elves kept apart, dwarves traded with it and despised it.
- **The Great Binding** (40 BU to 0). The last Thaumarch-Queen, **Ysarre the Bright**, forged the **Seven Lanterns**,
  one per House, to bind the great gods into service, so that the empire would never need to pray.
- **The Unmaking** (0 RY). The night the Binding was to close, the gods answered. Ishavel burned to glass in one night;
  the Thaumarchs died or vanished; the great works failed all at once (towers fell, roads cracked, brass servants
  stopped). The heartland became **the Glass Waste**. The Lanterns were scattered.
- **The Turning Away.** Since the Unmaking the great gods are distant. They grant prayers through their churches but
  no longer walk or speak plainly. In the hollow they left, the **small gods** grew: spirits of places, fed by
  offerings, bargaining with mortals. Scholars argue whether small gods were always there or were born of the gods'
  absence; peasants don't care.
- **The Ash Centuries** (0 to about 300 RY). Famine, plague, dark winters, the old roads lost. Peoples moved: the
  Hold-folk raided south, the steppe clans came west to east and back, the surviving Thaumarch kin fled south.
- Magic since: learned only from scraps of Thaumarch writing, slowly, at a cost. Wizards are few, distrusted, often
  half-mad, and the churches watch them. Priests' miracles come from the gods, great or small, and are rare enough to
  be talked about.

## The great gods

Twelve. Each has a church, temples in the cities, holy days, and a month. Gods are distant; their churches are
political, rich and quarrelsome. Alignment is the church's usual one (AD&D-style, for clerics).

| God | Titles | Portfolio | Align | Symbol | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Aurel** | the Sun-Judge | sun, law, oaths, judgement | LN | a golden scale on a sun disc | The Sunward Church; state faith of the Theocracy; hostile to small gods. |
| **Velis** | the Grey Mother | death, memory, passage | N | a grey veil, a closed eye | Keeps the dead and the graveyards; feared and respected; her priests bury everyone, even enemies. |
| **Karsk** | the Red Hand | war, strength, courage | N | a red open hand | Soldiers, mercenaries, the Free Companies; honour among killers. |
| **Marrah** | of the Deep, the Drowning Queen | the sea, storms, sailors | N | a wave and a hook | Capricious; sailors pay her and curse her. |
| **Hollin** | Lord of Sheaves | harvest, hearth, plenty | NG | a sheaf bound in red | The peasant's god; village shrines; humble priests. |
| **Sere** | the Veiled Eye | magic, secrets, knowledge | N | an eye behind a lattice | Patron of scholars and the few wizards; blamed for the Thaumarchy. |
| **Fennick** | the Lucky, the Rogue | luck, thieves, travellers, trade | CN | a coin on its edge | Merchants and thieves pray to him alike. |
| **Isaine** | the Bright Singer | love, beauty, song, spring | CG | a lark | Poets, lovers, players; the spring festival. |
| **Vorm** | the Winter King | winter, mountains, endurance | N | a white crown | The Hold-folk's high god; dwarves honour him too. |

## The small gods

Bound to one place: a river ford, a hill, a well, a sewer, a glacier. Strong on their own ground and weak away from
it; their priests (where they have any) lose their gifts a few days' walk from home. They grow with offerings, starve
when forgotten, can be bargained with, bribed, tricked, and (rarely) killed. Most people deal with small gods every
day and with the great gods at festivals, births and deaths. The Sunward Church calls small gods demons and burns
their shrines; Hollin's and Velis's priests tolerate them; Fennick's people bargain with them.

Named small gods of the first core:
- **The Ferryman** of Hask Ford. Older than Hask. Takes a coin or a toe from every crossing (custom: the coin);
  drowns anyone who builds a bridge over the Ambre at Hask. Hask's guilds pay him a yearly tithe on **Ferry Night**.
- **Mother Gutter** of Hask's sewers. Protects beggars, lost children and rat-catchers; enemy of the Open Mouth.
- **The Hill That Listens**, a barrow-hill in Calder's downs. Answers one question a year, for a price in secrets.
- **The Drowned Bell**, a sunken temple bell off the Drowned Coast. Rings before wrecks; pirates pray to it.
- **The Glass Saint**, a mirage in the Glass Waste that leads the lost to water, or to their deaths.
- **Grandmother Frost**, a glacier in the Holds who keeps the dead of the Hold-folk in her ice.
- **The Lady of the Weir**, the god of a weir on the Ambre above Hask; jealous of the Ferryman.
- **Old Tallow**, the god of one candle-makers' street in Hask (Tallow Lane): a fat, greedy, kindly god of small light.

Other land:
- **The Hornwall**, a mountain chain along the north (450–1250, 700–790), between the Holds and the south. The dwarven
  hold of **Deephorn** (820,750). Orc tribes in its eastern end serve the Margrave.
- **The Thornwold**, the elves' forest between Calder and Hask (600–760, 480–700). Its lady is **Ysmeine**.
- **The Ambre**, the great river: from the Hornwall at (800,760) south past Hask (800,520) through the Lantern
  Principalities to the Sunder Sea at (860,160). The Hask ferries are its only crossing for a hundred miles.
- **The Pale Tower**, a Thaumarch tower a day upriver from Hask (790,560), whiter than bone, leaning; under it the
  **Undercroft**, a dungeon of many levels, Hask's great place for adventurers to dig, die and get rich.
- **Redmere**, a marshy lake and old battlefield on the Calder–Margravate border (900,620).

## Hask

About 38,000 souls on both banks of the Ambre at **Hask Ford**, where the old Thaumarch road crossed. **There is no
bridge**: the Ferryman drowns bridge-builders (three tries in the city's history, the last in 1032). All crossings are by
the licensed ferries of the **Ferrymen's Guild**; a crossing costs one copper "toe" (the coin is stamped with a foot).

- **Rule.** The **Ford Council** of nine guildmasters elects the **Lord Warden**: **Benedek Sallow**, fat, clever,
  corrupt, and very good at keeping Hask out of wars. The **Watch** is underpaid and bribable.
- **Wards.** **Tollside** (east bank: guildhalls, merchant houses, the Council Hall); **Wetside** (west bank: docks,
  tanneries, poor); **the Stews** (Wetside's south end: brothels, gambling dens, the Quiet Hand); **the Lamp Quarter**
  (the Collegium, booksellers, alchemists); **Templegate** (the great temples of the twelve, Aurel's the newest and
  largest); **Tallow Lane** (candle-makers, Old Tallow's street); **Gallows Hill** (the Watch-house and the gibbets);
  **the Old Cut** (a Thaumarch canal, half silted).
- **Taverns.** **The Drowned Toe** (Wetside, the adventurers' tavern: crews meet, maps are sold, the Undercroft's dead
  are toasted); **the Gilded Eel** (Tollside, merchants); **Mother Kettle's** (the Stews, cheap and dangerous).
- **Factions in Hask.** The **Quiet Hand** (thieves' guild; head: **the Widow**); the **Collegium of the Lamp** (seven
  wizards, licensed and watched; head **Ilse Varrow**); the **merchant houses** (**Tesk**: river trade; **Orlemont**:
  money-lending; **Velloure**: southern spice and silk); the **House of the Scale** (the Sunward mission, Inquisitor
  **Magdal Crane**); the **Ember Court**'s spies (the Margrave's); the **Open Mouth** (Nhul's cult, in the sewers,
  hunted by Mother Gutter's beggars).
- **Adventurers** in Hask are a trade, not a calling: crews dig in the Undercroft, guard caravans, hunt bounties, and
  sell what they find to the Collegium or House Orlemont's brokers. Respectable folk think them one step from bandits.

## Organisations

- **The Sunward Church** (Aurel): hierarchy of the Theocracy and missions everywhere; inquisitors hunt wizards, small
  gods and Nhul cultists, in that order of zeal.
- **The Quiet Hand**: Hask's thieves' guild; licences thieves, fences, beggars' kings; at war with no one openly.
- **The Collegium of the Lamp**: Hask's licensed wizards; buy Thaumarch writings; quarrel over every scrap.
- **The Ferrymen's Guild**: Hask's oldest guild; pays the Ferryman; richest of the nine.
- **The Ember Court**: the Margrave's household of spies, assassins and tutors in the cities of the Reaches.
- **The Open Mouth**: the hidden cult of Nhul; feeds the Mouth with the living; cells in every city's underside.
- **The Free Companies**: mercenary companies of Karsk's soldiers; the best known **the Red Lanterns** and **the
  Ninefold Company**; they fight for Calder, the Lantern princes, anyone who pays.
- **The Grey Legions**: the Margrave's army: human levies, orc tribes of the Hornwall, and officers who obey without
  fear because they are more afraid of him.
- **The Glass-Pickers**: guides, scavengers and diggers of the Glass Waste, a loose brotherhood with its own oaths.
- **The Hold Moot**: the jarls' yearly gathering at Skarnholm, where feuds are judged and raids planned.

## People

- **Humans** are most of the Reaches: **Calderish** (fair, broad, western farmers and knights), **Haskers** (mixed,
  quick-tongued), **Hold-folk** (tall, pale, red or flaxen, raiders and skalds), the **steppe clans** (horse people,
  dark, tattooed), **Lanterners** (olive-skinned, dark-haired, pale-eyed, Thaumarch blood, proud of it),
  **Southrons** (the Greenmaw coast and the Sunder Sea ports: dark, quick, sailors and traders).
- **Elves**: few, old, strange, aloof; in the Thornwold and farther forests; they remember the Thaumarchy and refused
  it; they do not explain themselves.
- **Dwarves**: holds in the Hornwall; dour, proud, crafters, long memories and longer grudges; Asheth and Vorm.
- **Halflings**: the **Riverfolk** along the Ambre; boatmen, millers, smugglers; many work Hask's ferries.
- **Gnomes**: hill-folk in Calder's downs; tinkers and gem-cutters; on friendly terms with small gods.
- **Orcs**: tribes in the Hornwall's east and the Margravate; half-orcs common in Hask's Wetside and the Free Companies.

## Items

- **The Seven Lanterns**: the Thaumarch binding-lamps, one per House; scattered at the Unmaking. Each Lantern prince
  claims to hold one; (DM) at most two are real, and one lies under the Pale Tower.
- **The Ember Crown**: the Margrave's relic; keeps its wearer alive; (DM) it holds the trapped spark of a lesser
  power, and it is burning out.
- **Ferryman's toes**: Hask's copper crossing-coins, stamped with a foot; given as luck charms.
- **Brass servants**: the stopped Thaumarch automatons found in ruins; (DM) some wake.

## Holidays

- **The Hollow Days**: the five days at the year's turn, outside the months; unlucky, Nhul's in folk belief; doors
  shut, fires kept, no journeys begun.
- **Isaine's Morning**: 1 Isaine, the spring festival, songs, courtship, the first day of the campaign year.
- **The Sun's Judgement**: 15 Aurelmas, midsummer; Aurel's churches hold courts and sentence the year's heretics.
- **Harvest Home**: the last day of Hollin; Hollin's feast; small gods given the first loaf too.
- **Ferry Night**: 1 Oruun, in Hask: the guilds pay the Ferryman's tithe at the Ford by lantern-light.

## The calendar

Twelve months of 30 days, named for the great gods, and the five **Hollow Days** that open the year: 365 days.
Order: the Hollow Days (Midwinter), **Vormas**, **Velith** (Winter), **Isaine**, **Ashen**, **Marrah** (Spring),
**Aurelmas**, **Karsk**, **Fennick** (Summer), **Hollin**, **Oruun**, **Seremark** (Autumn), **the Nameless Month**
(Winter; once Nhul's, its name is not said). Era **RY**. A date reads "4 Isaine 1188 RY".

## Tone and writing

- Concrete, sensory and wry. Name a smell, a coin, a custom. Let places have odd rules (Vance), let cities be crooked
  and alive (Leiber), let the wild be old and cruel (Howard), let gods and fate be playful and cold (Moorcock), all in
  plain modern English without archaisms or "thee".
- Every page gives the DM something to use at the table: a rumour, a custom, a danger, a price.
- Player-visible text is what a well-travelled local could tell; secrets, true causes and plot hooks go in `::: dm`
  sections.
