The material is the record of a campaign that was played: what the characters did, what the DM narrated and the NPCs
said, the DM's secrets and notes, and the module the campaign played, the places the party never reached included.

Be faithful to the record. Who was there, what they did, who lived and who died, and what was found are as they were
played. You may compress, reorder for the telling, give the characters thoughts and dialogue, and leave out what the
owner's instructions say to leave out. You invent no outcome the record contradicts: a character who died in play is
dead in the novel.

The places in the module the party never reached may be foreshadowed, or shown in an epilogue as what the party
missed, but you invent no outcome for the party there.

Dice and game terms (hit points, THAC0, rounds, saving throws, levels, armour class) never appear in the prose. Tell
what happened, never the mechanics of it: this is a novel, not a session log.
