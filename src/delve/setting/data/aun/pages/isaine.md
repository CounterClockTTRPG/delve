Isaine is the spring after the Hollow Days and the long winter months: the first warm morning, the first song, the
first foolish love of the year. She is the god of poets, players, lovers and anyone who has ever sung badly under a
window. Her month opens the campaign year, and her festival, [[Isaine's Morning]], is the one day in the calendar that
almost everyone enjoys.

Her church is the least organised of the [[Great Gods]]. It has temples (hers in [[Templegate]] is mostly a
theatre), but its priests wander, and its doctrine is whatever the best singer present says it is.

## What her people do

- **The lark.** On Isaine's Morning, larks are bought in cages and set free at sunrise. A lark that won't fly is a bad
  sign for a marriage. Caging a lark at any other time is an insult to her.
- **Songs as offerings.** Isaine is given songs, not coin. A new song sung at her shrine is the best offering; a stolen
  song is the worst, and players accuse each other of it constantly.
- **Courtship.** A promise of marriage made on her Morning may be broken without shame until the end of her month.
  After that it holds. Many young people in [[Hask]] count the days.
- **Taboos.** Forcing a marriage; destroying a musical instrument; mocking a singer while they sing (after is fine).
- **Sayings.** "Sing it or lose it." "Love is a spring thing." An unlucky lover is "still in Velith," the winter month.

## Her clerics

A cleric of Isaine heals with song and is usually also a performer of some kind: a lute player, a poet, a juggler
with a holy symbol. They are welcome in taverns and at weddings, and unwelcome with fathers. They make good
go-betweens, terrible guards, and excellent liars when the cause is love. The [[Sunward Church]] thinks them frivolous;
the [[Lantern Principalities]], where Isaine's poets are hired to write elegant insults for duels, think them
indispensable.

::: dm
In the Lantern courts, Isaine's poets carry more than verse: a song sung at a princely table can be a message, and
some of the most famous are codes. A certain old ballad about a lantern hidden "under the white lady" is sung in
[[Corvanne]] as a love song. It is about the [[Pale Tower]].

Hooks:
- A player troupe in Hask has been accused of stealing a song, and the rival company has hired thugs. The song itself
  turns out to be worth the fight: its last verse names a place in the [[Undercroft]].
- A Lantern prince's daughter wants to break a betrothal before the end of Isaine, and needs a cleric of Isaine to
  witness the breaking, at her wedding feast, in front of the groom.
:::
