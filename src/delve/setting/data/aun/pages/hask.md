Hask is the loudest place in the Reaches. Thirty-eight thousand people live on the two banks of the [[Ambre]] where
the old Thaumarch road comes down to the water and stops, because there is no bridge and there never will be. Every
cart, cow, pilgrim and army that wants to cross the river for a hundred miles has to come here, queue, haggle, and pay
a copper to a ferryman. Hask has grown fat on that copper for four hundred years and learned to sell everything else
while the travellers wait.

It smells of river mud, tannery lime, tallow smoke and money. It is crooked in both senses: lanes that bend because a
guild once owned the corner, and a government that bends for anyone with silver. The nine guildmasters of the
[[Free City of Hask|Ford Council]] and their Lord Warden, [[Benedek Sallow]], rule it by day; the [[Widow]] and her
[[Quiet Hand]] by night; [[Ilse Varrow]] of the wizards and the inquisitor [[Magdal Crane]] watch both. Respectable
people in [[Aldmoor]] and [[Candor]] call it a sink. Respectable people in Hask call it home and lock their doors.

## The lie of the city

The Ambre here is a quarter-mile of brown water, slow in summer and vicious in the spring melt. In the middle of it,
at [[Hask Ford]], the stumps of three failed bridges stick out of the current like bad teeth.

**The east bank** is the rich one, built on a low rise above the floods:

- **[[Tollside]]**: guildhalls, merchant houses, the Council Hall, the money-tables. Stone, glass windows, clean
  steps, a Watch that actually patrols.
- **[[Templegate]]**: the hill north of Tollside where the twelve great gods keep their temples, Aurel's the newest,
  whitest and largest.
- **Gallows Hill**: the Watch-house, the price-boards of the city's fines, and the gibbets, where the crows are fat.

**The west bank** is the busy one, built on whatever didn't wash away:

- **[[Wetside]]**: docks, warehouses, tanneries, rope-walks and the poor. Half-orcs, Riverfolk, deserters, and
  anyone who arrived yesterday. The [[Drowned Toe]] is here.
- **[[Stews|The Stews]]**: Wetside's southern end, where the river bends and the law gives up. Dice, brothels,
  pit-fights and the Quiet Hand.
- **[[Lamp Quarter|The Lamp Quarter]]**: the north end, along the Old Cut, where the [[Collegium of the Lamp]]
  keeps its wizards and the booksellers keep their prices high. One Thaumarch street-lamp still burns here, as it has
  for twelve hundred years.
- **[[Tallow Lane]]**: a single crooked street of candle-makers running up from the docks to the Lamp Quarter,
  sacred to [[Old Tallow]].

**The Old Cut** is a Thaumarch canal that once ran straight as a ruled line from the river to somewhere nobody
remembers. Now it is half silted, crossed by plank walks, and dredged by debt-gangs. Things come up in the dredgers'
buckets: bones, brass, once a door.

## Getting across

A crossing costs one copper **toe**, the [[Ferryman's Toe|coin stamped with a foot]]. The licensed ferries of the
[[Ferrymen's Guild]] run from the Toll Stair on the east bank to the Wet Stair on the west, dawn to dusk, and by
lantern at a higher price after. Riverfolk halflings crew most of them. A horse is three toes, a cart five, a coffin
seven (the [[Ferryman]] likes the dead). Swimming across is legal only if you throw a toe into the water first, and
locals still won't do it.

Everyone in Hask carries a few toes for luck; ferry-mates read fortunes in the way they land.

## Street life

Hask never quite sleeps; it changes shifts.

- **Dawn**: the ferry-horns at the Toll Stair, the tanneries' lime vats steaming, beggars taking their pitches by
  rights that are older than the guilds. [[Mother Gutter]]'s children own every gutter-grating; step over them.
- **Morning**: carters queue at the Wet Stair, sometimes for a day. The queue is a market of its own: pie-sellers,
  pickpockets, sergeants recruiting for the [[Free Companies]], and men selling places in it they do not own.
- **Noon**: the money-tables open on Tollside; the bell of the Council Hall rings the toll prices of the day, which
  change. Criers read the fines. Every fourth day somebody is hanged.
- **Evening**: the Lamp Quarter lights its single ancient lamp; the Stews light everything else. Crews come back from
  the [[Pale Tower]] by the towpath with mud to the knees and something wrapped in a cloak.
- **Night**: the Watch walks Tollside in pairs and Wetside not at all. The roofs belong to the Quiet Hand; the
  sewers, people whisper, to something worse than Mother Gutter.

## Customs

- **Small gods first.** Haskers visit the great temples on holy days and the small gods every day: a crumb for Mother
  Gutter at a grating, a candle-stub for Old Tallow, a toe for the Ferryman. Skip it and strangers will tell you so.
- **No bridges, not even small ones.** Children are taught not to lay a plank across a puddle, and a joke about a
  bridge in a ferry queue gets you thrown out of it.
- **The fine is the law.** Hask doesn't ask whether you did it; it asks whether you can pay. Bargaining with a Watch
  sergeant over a fine is expected. Paying the posted price is a sign you're new.
- **A crew is a trade.** Adventurers are licensed, taxed and despised like any other trade. A crew registers its name
  at the Watch-house for two oars a season, which buys the right to carry swords inside the city and the certainty that
  the Watch knows who to blame.

## Things to do in Hask

- **Find a crew, sell a map, hear the dead toasted** at the Drowned Toe.
- **Delve** in the [[Undercroft]] beneath the Pale Tower, a day upriver by the towpath, past the [[Lady of the Weir]]'s
  water.
- **Sell what you find** to the Collegium, which pays badly and asks questions, or to House Orlemont's brokers on
  Tollside, who pay worse and don't.
- **Guard a caravan** east to the [[Margravate]] or west to [[Kingdom of Calder|Calder]] for Houses Tesk, Orlemont or
  Velloure, who are always hiring and never pay in advance.
- **Collect a bounty** off the boards on Gallows Hill: river pirates, a Collegium apprentice who ran off with a book,
  a rat as big as a dog that the rat-catchers won't touch.
- **Lose it all** in the Stews, at Mother Kettle's, where the stew is cheap and the knives are free.
- **Watch the city's year**: [[Isaine's Morning]] with its songs on the ferries, and [[Ferry Night]] in autumn, when
  the guilds row out by lantern-light and pay the Ferryman his tithe while the whole city holds its breath.

::: dm
**The city under the city.** Hask's sewers are Thaumarch work, cut far deeper than a town of this size needs, and they
connect in places to the old drains of the Old Cut and, through those, to the upper reaches of the Undercroft. Mother
Gutter knows every pipe. So does the [[Open Mouth]], whose cell has been growing for twenty years in the dark and
has lately learned to take beggars without anyone noticing, except Mother Gutter, who has noticed.

**The lamp that never goes out** in the Lamp Quarter is fed from below. Its light dims a little each time something
of the Thaumarchy is carried out of the Pale Tower. Ilse Varrow has measured it; she tells nobody.

**Hooks.**
- *The queue.* A carter at the Wet Stair has been in the queue for three days because every ferry turns him away. His
  cart holds a box sealed with Margravate wax, and the ferrymen say the Ferryman doesn't want it on his river.
- *A missing beggar-child.* Mother Gutter's beggars will pay in secrets, which in Hask is better than gold, for the
  crew that finds where the children are going.
- *Ferry Night.* This year the Lord Warden's election falls on the same night as the tithe. If the tithe is short, or
  late, or stolen, the Ferryman will take his due from the ferries, and everyone knows it.
