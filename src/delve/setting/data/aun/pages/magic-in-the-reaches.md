In the [[Thaumarchy]], magic lit the streets. Today most people will go their whole lives without seeing a spell cast,
and would rather keep it that way.

## Wizards

There are no wizard schools, no spell shops and no village wise man who can mend a broken leg with a word. A wizard
learns from scraps: a page of Thaumarch script dug out of a ruin, a cracked tablet, a dead wizard's notebook bought at
a ruinous price. Every spell is a translation of a language nobody speaks, and a mistake in it can cost an eye or a
mind. Wizards are few, secretive, often a little mad, and almost always poor until suddenly they are not.

The one licensed body of wizards in the Reaches is the [[Collegium of the Lamp]] in [[Hask]]: seven of them, under
[[Ilse Varrow]], who buy every Thaumarch scrap that comes up from the [[Undercroft]] and quarrel over each one.
Elsewhere a wizard is tolerated by a lord who finds them useful, hunted by the [[Sunward Church]], or both in turn.

Common folk believe a wizard's shadow falls the wrong way, that they cannot cross running water without paying double,
and that they are to blame for the [[Unmaking]]. The first two are untrue.

## Priests

A priest's miracle comes from a god, great or small, and a true miracle is rare enough that a village talks about it
for a generation. Most priests never work one. Those who do serve the [[Great Gods]] through their churches, or the
[[Small Gods]] in their own place: a small god's priest loses their gifts a few days' walk from home.

## Old things

The high magic of the Thaumarchy returns only as things dug up: a lamp that will not go out, a blade that cuts
shadows, a [[Brass Servants|brass servant]] that twitches when spoken to. Every such find is worth a fortune and a
knife in the back. The [[Seven Lanterns]] and the [[Ember Crown]] are the famous ones.

## The law

- In the [[Sunward Theocracy]], casting a spell without a licence from the Church is heresy; the Church issues no
  licences.
- In Hask, a wizard must hold a Collegium seal, a small brass disc worn at the throat; unsealed casting in the city is
  fined fifty gold the first time and the gallows the second.
- In the [[Margravate]], wizards serve the Margrave or vanish.

::: dm
Magic should feel dangerous and precious. A scroll is treasure; a spellbook is a feud. When a wizard PC learns a new
spell from a found scrap, let it take days of work and a small price: a bad night's dreams from [[Oruun]], a tremor
in the hand, a nosebleed whenever the spell is cast near Thaumarch ruins.

- The Collegium pays well for scraps but copies them before haggling; a crew that sells one page may find the same
  spell being cast against them a month later.
- Inquisitor [[Magdal Crane]] keeps a list of every unsealed caster seen in Hask. A PC wizard will be on it within a
  week.
- Some Thaumarch spells were written to draw on the gods' bound power. Cast today, they still reach for it, and
  something sometimes answers.
:::
