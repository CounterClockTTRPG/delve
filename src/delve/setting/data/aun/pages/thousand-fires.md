The Thousand Fires is where the steppe clans of the [[Horse Steppe]] gather each autumn: a wide, shallow bowl of
grass in the middle of the plains, with a spring at its centre that has never been known to fail. For most of the year
it is empty but for a few herders and the stones. For a month after [[Harvest Home]] it is the largest town in the
west, made of felt, smoke and horses.

## The place

- **The Bowl.** A grass hollow some three miles across. Each clan has its own place in it, by long custom; a clan that
  camps in another's place is starting a war.
- **The Mother Well.** The spring at the centre, ringed with flat stones carved with the marks of every clan that
  ever came. It is a small god, and no one may draw from it but children.
- **The Fires.** On the first night of the gathering, every clan, family and lone rider lights a fire around the rim of
  the Bowl. Riders swear there are a thousand. No one has counted, because counting them is bad luck.
- **The Khan's Ground.** A low mound on the west side, where the khan's tent stands and the chiefs meet to judge,
  choose and quarrel.
- **The Platforms.** North of the Bowl, a field of sky-burial platforms, old and new. The birds over it are heavy.

## The gathering

- **The peace of the fires.** For the month of the gathering no blood may be shed on the grass of the Bowl. Feuds are
  suspended, raiders and their victims camp side by side, and quarrels are settled by horse races, archery contests and
  the khan's judgement.
- **The horse fair.** Calderish dealers, Hold-folk traders and Hask agents come to buy horses. They camp outside the
  Bowl, pay the khan a gift for the right to enter, and are watched.
- **The choosing.** If the khan is dead, or the chiefs are tired of him, a new khan is chosen on the last night.
  Ulagh Khan has been confirmed eleven years running.
- **Marriages.** Most marriages between clans are made here, and so most courtship, and so most fights that are not
  quite bloodshed.

## At the table

The Thousand Fires is a gathering of everyone, under a peace that everyone wants to break. Adventurers come to buy
horses, to carry a message to the khan, to follow someone who thinks the peace will hide him, or to find out who is
about to break it.

::: dm
**This autumn** the [[Ember Court]]'s envoy will ask Ulagh Khan, in front of the chiefs, to lead the clans east. The
khan means to refuse in public and accept in private. A chief who wants to be khan means to have the envoy killed on
the grass of the Bowl, and blame a Calderish dealer, breaking the peace and Calder's trade in one stroke.

**Hooks.**
- A Calderish horse-dealer hires the party as guards and go-betweens. He does not know he has been chosen as the
  scapegoat.
- A child has drawn water from the Mother Well and brought up a brass key. Three clans say it is theirs.
- A chief's father died in [[Aldmoor]] and was buried whole in a Calderish churchyard. The clan will pay a horse a
  day until his body is dug up and brought to the platforms; Velis's priests in Aldmoor will not allow it.
:::
