The **Seven Years' War** (1104–1111 RY) was fought by the [[Kingdom of Calder]] and the [[Sunward Theocracy]] against
the [[Margravate]] of the Undying Margrave, [[Teodric Vael]]. Nobody won. Many people in their late eighties still
remember it, and every Calderish village has a stone with names on it.

## How it began

The Margravate had pushed its border west for decades, one village at a time. In 1104 a Calderish baron hanged a
Margravate tax collector from his own cart. The Margrave's answer was an army. Calder called on the Theocracy, which
had its own reasons: it wanted the Margrave burned as a sorcerer, since a man does not live two centuries without
something unholy on his head.

## The war

- Seven summers of raids and sieges along the march, much of it in the downs and the marsh country.
- Calder's knights won the open battles and lost the war of attrition; the Margravate's [[Grey Legions]] did not
  break, did not tire and did not seem to fear death.
- The [[Free Companies]] fought on both sides, sometimes in the same year. The Ninefold Company took its name in this
  war, from the nine of its captains who survived it.
- [[Hask]], under the Lord Warden of the time, declared itself neutral and sold grain, boats and swords to everyone.
  This is remembered in Calder, bitterly, and in Hask, fondly.

## Redmere

In 1110 the armies met at [[Redmere]], the marshy lake on the border. The fight went on for a day and a night in water
to the knee. When it ended neither side could hold the field, and both armies withdrew from a lake that stank of the
dead for a year. The next spring, both sides signed a truce at a crossroads inn, the border back roughly where it had
been. It has held for seventy-seven years, more or less.

## What it left

- Calder spent its treasury and its young knights, and has not recovered either.
- The Theocracy learned that its inquisitors cannot burn what they cannot reach.
- The Margrave lost nothing that mattered to him.
- At Redmere, on still nights, fishermen say the drowned still march.

::: dm
The truce is a paper wall. In 1187 the Grey Legions mustered on the Calder march again, and in 1188 [[Anselm IX]] wants
a holy war to make his name. Veterans of Redmere are old now, but they remember things the histories left out.

- A Calderish banner lost at Redmere is still in the marsh, with the old king's seal sewn into it; a baron would pay a
  fortune for it to back a claim to the throne.
- The Margrave's officers at Redmere held because they feared him more than the enemy. One of them, now very old, has
  deserted to Hask and drinks at the [[Drowned Toe]]; he knows how the Margrave keeps that fear, and someone wants him
  dead before he tells.
:::
