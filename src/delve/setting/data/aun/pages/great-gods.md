The **great gods** are twelve. Each has a church, temples in the cities, a holy day and a month of the year. Once they
walked close and spoke plainly; since the [[Unmaking]] they keep their distance. They still grant prayers through
their priests, but nobody alive has seen one, and the churches that speak for them are political, rich and given to
quarrelling with each other over precedence, land and the order of processions.

## The Twelve

| [[Nhul]] | the Nameless | rot, hunger, devouring (forbidden) |

## How people deal with them

Ordinary folk meet the great gods at festivals, births, weddings and burials. A farmer prays to [[Hollin]] for rain and
leaves bread at the ford for its [[Small Gods|small god]] the same morning. A soldier wears [[Karsk]]'s red hand and a
[[Ferryman's Toe]] in his boot. Nobody worships only one god except priests, and not all of them.

The months are named for them (see [[Calendar of the Reckoning]]), save the last, which was once [[Nhul]]'s and whose
name nobody says.

## The churches

- The [[Sunward Church]] of Aurel is the largest, the state faith of the [[Sunward Theocracy]], and the only one that
  claims the others serve under its god.
- [[Velis]]'s grey priests bury everyone, friend or foe, and are left alone even by armies.
- [[Sere]]'s temples are the poorest in any city; people blame Sere for the [[Thaumarchy]].
- The temples of all twelve stand side by side in Hask's [[Templegate]], where processions collide twice a year.

::: dm
The gods are not dead, only withdrawn, and they remember the [[Great Binding]]. They answer prayers coldly and keep
their own counsel. When a god does act, it should feel like weather: large, impersonal, not quite aimed.

- A PC cleric who asks the same favour twice in a day may get an answer meant for someone else.
- The churches all want any [[Seven Lanterns|Lantern]] found, and each would destroy it rather than let a rival keep
  it, except the Sunward Church, which wants to use it.
:::
