**Ferry Night** falls on 1 Oruun, in autumn, and is kept only in [[Hask]]. On this night the city's guilds pay the
[[Ferryman]], the small god of [[Hask Ford]], his yearly tithe, by lantern-light, at the water's edge.

## The tithe

At sunset the nine guildmasters of the Ford Council walk down to the Ford from the Council Hall in [[Tollside]], each
followed by a servant carrying a covered chest. The [[Ferrymen's Guild]] goes first, as the oldest. The chests hold
the tithe: a tenth of a tenth of the year's tolls, in copper toes (see [[Ferryman's Toe]]), never silver or gold. The
Lord Warden, [[Benedek Sallow]], stands at the end of the landing stage and reads the sum aloud.

Then the chests are loaded onto the guild barges, the only boats allowed on the water that night, and rowed out to
midstream. They are tipped into the [[Ambre]] one by one, and every lantern in the city is lit at once. If the river
takes the coin quietly, the year is good. If it boils, or if anything comes up, there is a meeting of the Council
before dawn.

## The night

- Every boat on the river carries a lantern. From the [[Pale Tower]] upriver the city looks like it is on fire.
- Haskers throw their own toes in, for luck, for a lover, for a curse on someone.
- No ferry runs from sunset to dawn, and nothing crosses but the tithe barges. The ferrymen sit in their moored boats
  with lanterns lit and wait.
- [[Wetside]] holds a fair along the docks: eel pies, dice, and a great deal of drinking.
- Nobody says the word "bridge". Saying it on Ferry Night costs a round of drinks at the [[Drowned Toe]], or a beating
  elsewhere.

## At the table

Ferry Night is Hask's loudest night and its most watched: every guild, every faction and every thief is out. It is
an excellent night for a crime, and everyone knows it.

::: dm
In 1188 the tithe is short. A guildmaster has been skimming for three years, and the Ferryman knows. This year the
river will not take the coin quietly, and the Lord Warden's election falls on the same night.

- The [[Quiet Hand]] plans to rob one of the tithe chests between the Council Hall and the Ford; the [[Widow]] does not
  intend to keep the money, only to see whose chest is light.
- [[Magdal Crane]] watches the rite from a rooftop and takes notes: she is building a case to burn the Ferryman.
- The [[Lady of the Weir]] sends something downriver on Ferry Night every year to spoil the rite. This year it is
  bigger.
:::
