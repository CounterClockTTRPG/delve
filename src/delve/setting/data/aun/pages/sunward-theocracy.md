The Theocracy is the south-west of the Reaches: a hot, dry, orderly country of olive terraces, salt pans, white towns
and sun-temples, ruled from [[Candor]] by the Hierarch of [[Aurel]]. It began as a coastal church around 500 RY and
became a state in 612, when Hierarch **Anselm I** took the crown of the south-west for himself and his successors.
Every Hierarch since has taken the name Anselm. The present one, [[Anselm IX]], was raised in 1186 at thirty-two, and
is the most dangerous man to hold the office in two hundred years.

## The land

Coastal plains along the Sunder Sea, behind them dry hills, and behind those the long valleys that run north into
[[Kingdom of Calder|Calder]]. The [[Drowned Coast]] lies to the west, beyond the salt marshes; the
[[Lantern Principalities]] lie east, past a line of hill forts. The roads are good and patrolled. The towns are clean.
Visitors notice the quiet first: no street singers, no fortune-tellers, no little shrines at the corners.

## Rule

The Hierarch rules through the [[Sunward Church]]: bishops govern the provinces, priests the parishes, and
**inquisitors** go wherever they please. Nobles exist but hold their land of the Church and their titles at its
pleasure. The law is Aurel's law, written down, read aloud each noon in every market square, and kept.

## Law and custom

- **Whitewash.** Every house, wall and barn is whitewashed each year before the [[Sun's Judgement]] on 15 Aurelmas. A
  grey house is a fine; a grey house two years running is a visit.
- **The noon bell.** Work stops at noon for the reading of the law. Oaths and contracts are sworn at noon, under the
  sun, on a brass scale. A bargain struck after sunset is void, which every travelling merchant learns once.
- **No small gods.** Small gods are demons in the Church's teaching. Their shrines are burned, their offerings are
  confiscated, and leaving a bowl of milk at a well is heresy of the third degree (a fine and a flogging). Inquisitors
  pay for information.
- **Wizards.** A spellcaster must register with the bishop's court, wear a grey cord at the wrist, and may not cast
  within a mile of a temple. Unregistered wizards are judged at the Sun's Judgement, and seldom go home.
- **Velis.** The Grey Mother's priests are permitted because someone has to bury the dead, and they bury the burned
  too.
- **Prices.** Bread is cheap and fixed by law; wine, 1 silver a jug; a licence to carry a sword, 5 silver a year.

## Neighbours

The Theocracy fought beside Calder in the [[Seven Years' War]] and thinks Calder owes it. It despises the Lantern
princes as sorcerers' grandchildren, and sends ships against the pirates of [[Gullhaven]] when the weather allows.
Its mission in [[Hask]], the House of the Scale under Inquisitor [[Magdal Crane]], is the largest temple in
[[Templegate]] and the most watched.

## At the table

The Theocracy is the place for a campaign about law, fear and quiet resistance. Adventurers who are not careful are
arrested within a week; adventurers who are careful find the whole country full of people who need help and dare not
ask for it.

::: dm
**What lies under the whitewash.** The small gods are not gone. In half the farms of the dry hills, a hearth-god or a
well-spirit lives behind the plaster, fed in secret with a crust on the Hollow Days. Families paint a tiny mark under
the whitewash to show where. Inquisitors know the mark, and sometimes leave it alone, for a price.

**Hooks.**
- A village asks the party to carry its well-god out of the Theocracy: a stone the size of a fist, which grows heavier
  each mile from home and stops being a god altogether if it gets too far.
- A registered wizard in Candor has been told she will be judged this summer. She pays well to be smuggled to the
  [[Collegium of the Lamp]], and she is carrying stolen Church records.
- Anselm IX wants a war, and needs a cause. Someone is going to burn a temple near the Calder border and blame the
  [[Margravate]]. The party may be hired to do it without knowing who hired them.
:::
