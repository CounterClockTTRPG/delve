A few miles up the towpath from [[Hask]], on the way to the [[Pale Tower]], the [[Ambre]] pours over a long, low weir
of black stones. Riverfolk halflings run mills along it and set eel-traps in its pools. The village is called Hurlock
Weir, and the weir has a god: the Lady.

## What she is like

The Lady of the Weir is the god of the still water above the stones and the white water below them. She is
beautiful, people say, the way deep water is beautiful in summer. She keeps the millraces turning, fills the
eel-traps and drowns a few people a year, mostly young men who came out at night because someone sang to them.

The millers of Hurlock Weir honour her with the first eel of the season, ribbons tied to the weir-posts, a garland of
rushes on [[Isaine's Morning]], and a widow's cap hung on the weir-posts for every year a mill stands. The Riverfolk
are fond of her and careful of her in equal parts.

## The Ferryman's rival

The Lady is jealous of the [[Ferryman]] downriver. He is honoured by a whole city; she has one village. He gets a tithe
on [[Ferry Night]]; she gets eels. Everyone on the river knows this, and the Riverfolk of Hurlock Weir pay the
Ferryman his toe at Hask with their eyes lowered, so the Lady won't see.

She makes it known, in her way. Barges slow at the weir and sometimes stick there; cargoes go missing; floods come
down early. A traveller on the towpath may meet a dripping woman on the stones who asks why anyone needs to cross the
river at Hask at all.

## Dealing with her

Travellers on the towpath to the Pale Tower pass the weir both ways. A crew that leaves her something (a fish, a
song, a bright thing from the [[Undercroft]]) will find the path dry and the mist thin. A crew that walks past with
its eyes on the ground will reach the tower wet, late, and shy one pack.

::: dm
The Lady wants a bridge. Not at Hask, at Hurlock Weir: a crossing on her water, her own toll, her own city one day.
She has been whispering to a Margravate engineer who wants a road west for an army, and the masons sinking pilings by
night above the weir are his. If the bridge stands, the Ferryman loses his monopoly and Hask loses its reason to be.
