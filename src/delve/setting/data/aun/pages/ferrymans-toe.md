A **toe** is [[Hask]]'s copper crossing-coin, stamped on one side with a bare foot and on the other with the city's
mark, a ferry-boat on three wavy lines. One toe buys a single crossing of the [[Ambre]] at [[Hask Ford]] on a licensed
boat of the [[Ferrymen's Guild]].

## Why a foot

The [[Ferryman]], the small god of the Ford, is older than the city. He has always taken a coin or a toe from everyone
who crosses. The custom is to give the coin. The stamp is a reminder of the alternative, and Haskers enjoy explaining
this to visitors.

## Value

A toe is worth a copper piece, more or less; changers in [[Tollside]] take them at par, and they pass as small change
up and down the river. Outside the [[Free City of Hask]] they are worth a little more than their copper, because
people want them as charms.

## As a charm

- Carried in the boot, a toe is luck on the road; it is the commonest charm in the Reaches.
- Crews going into the [[Undercroft]] drop a toe in the Ambre first. A crew that forgets is counted as already dead
  at the [[Drowned Toe]].
- A toe laid on a dead person's tongue pays their way across, whatever river it is.
- A toe given to a lover means "come back". A toe thrown at someone's feet means "go and drown".
- Bent toes are bad luck; nobody takes one in change.

## Custom

The tithe paid to the Ferryman on [[Ferry Night]] is always paid in toes, never silver or gold. Every guild holds a
chest of them for the purpose.

::: dm
The Ferryman knows every toe that has passed the Ford. A toe that has been paid to him and somehow recovered, from the
riverbed or a drowned man's purse, is marked: whoever carries it can cross the Ambre anywhere without a boat once, and
the Ferryman will come to collect a second fee.

- Counterfeit toes are minted in [[Wetside]] from lead dipped in copper. The Ferryman drowns the passer, not the minter;
  the minter is getting rich.
- A crew that finds an old toe in the [[Undercroft]] with the foot facing the other way has found a coin from before
  the [[Bought Freedom]], when the Ferryman had a different face.
:::
