West of [[Kingdom of Calder|Calder]] the trees give out, and then there is grass: knee-high, then waist-high, then
higher than a man on foot, for three hundred miles to the sea. This is the Horse Steppe, the grass sea, home of the
steppe clans. They are dark-haired, dark-eyed and tattooed, with a tattoo for every clan, deed and grief. They live in
felt tents, move with the seasons and their herds, and ride before they walk.

In the [[Ash Centuries]] the clans rode east across the starving Reaches, as far as the edge of the
[[Glass Waste]], and most of them rode back again; the songs say the grass called them home. There are thirty-odd clans, from great ones of four thousand tents to families of a dozen.
Once a year they gather at the [[Thousand Fires]]. The khan there now is [[Ulagh Khan]].

## The land

Flat, then rolling, then flat again. Shallow rivers wander in wide beds. There are few trees and fewer stones, and
every stone has a name. Wells are the wealth of the steppe and its law: a clan's wells are its own, and to foul one is
the one crime that every clan punishes alike. In summer the grass burns gold and the fires come; in winter the wind
comes off the Iron Sea and kills the old and the weak.

## Rule

Each clan has its chief, chosen by the riders. The khan is chosen at the Thousand Fires by the chiefs, and holds the
title for as long as the chiefs come when he calls. He judges disputes between clans, leads the great hunts and the
wars, and divides what Calder pays.

## Custom

- **Horses.** A rider owns three to ten horses. A gift of a horse is a bond; to refuse one is an insult; to ride
  another's horse without leave is theft and is punished as theft of a child.
- **Sky-burial.** The dead are laid on high wooden platforms on the open grass, for the birds. The bones are gathered
  after a year and buried by a well. Steppe folk find the Calderish habit of putting the dead in the ground whole
  disgusting.
- **Tattoos.** A clan mark on the neck; a deed on the arm; a dead friend on the back. Old riders are covered in them.
  Reading a stranger's tattoos aloud is rude, and also how the clans find out who you are.
- **Small gods.** The steppe's gods are its wells, its named stones and its winds. A rider pours a mouthful of milk on
  a stone in passing and asks it the way. [[Karsk]] is honoured by the war-bands; [[Velis]] is thanked at the platforms.
- **The peace of the guest.** A stranger who rides in openly, alone or with few, and dismounts at the edge of a camp, is
  under the clan's protection until he leaves. One who rides in at a gallop is a raider.
- **Prices.** A steppe horse, 20 to 60 gold in Calder, a quarter of that on the grass; a sack of Calderish grain in a
  bad winter, a horse.

## Neighbours

Calder buys horses, sells grain and iron, pays the clans not to raid, and is raided anyway by clans who were not paid.
Calder's western marches are full of border keeps, half-steppe families and old grudges. Hold-folk longships raid the
river mouths in the north. The [[Margravate]] is a long way east, and lately its gold has been much nearer.

## At the table

The steppe is good for chases, hunts, horse-trading, wells to defend, clan feuds and long rides with nothing in sight.
Adventurers are outsiders here and stay alive by knowing the customs; a party that fouls a well will be hunted by
everyone.

**The Margrave's gold.** An [[Ember Court]] envoy has been at Ulagh Khan's fires since last autumn, offering gold
and iron for the clans to ride east of Calder's border keeps when the Grey Legions march. Some chiefs want it. The khan
has not said no.

**Hooks.**
- A clan's well has been poisoned and the clan blames a Calderish border lord. The party has three days to find the
  real culprit before the war-bands ride.
- A named stone has been stolen and carried into Calder, where a baron uses it as a mounting block. The stone's clan
  wants it back, and the stone, a small god, is not happy either.
- A Calderish horse-dealer hires guards for a drive of three hundred horses to Aldmoor. Two clans claim the herd.
