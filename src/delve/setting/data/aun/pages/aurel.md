Aurel weighs. That is the whole of his creed, and his priests can talk about it for a day without stopping. Every oath
is put on his scale, every judgement made in his light, and a thing done in shadow is half a crime already. He is the
god of the [[Sunward Church]], the state faith of the [[Sunward Theocracy]], and the newest and richest of the
[[Great Gods]] in the cities: his temple in [[Templegate]] is the largest in [[Hask]], and was paid for with fines.

## What his people do

- **Oaths at noon.** A serious oath is sworn at midday, out of doors, with the right hand held flat to the sun. An oath
  sworn under a roof or after dark is, in Sunward law, only a promise.
- **The weighing.** At the temple door the faithful put a coin on a brass scale against a little sun-stamped weight.
  If the coin is light (clipped, shaved, false) the priest keeps it and fines the giver. Sunward cities have the
  honest coin in the Reaches and the most frightened money-changers.
- **Taboos.** Breaking an oath; lying in daylight; burying the dead facing away from the sunrise; making an offering to
  a small god. The last is the one that gets people burned.
- **Sayings.** "The sun sees the scale." "Swear at noon, hang at noon." A Sunward judge ends every sentence with "It is
  weighed," and nobody in the court speaks again until he stands.

## His clerics

A cleric of Aurel is a judge before a healer. They carry a small balance on a chain, wear white and gold, and are
trained in law as much as prayer; most can quote the Sunward Codex from memory. They may be called on to settle a
quarrel anywhere they go, and are expected to. They do not lie, will not break an oath even to an enemy, and look on
[[Small Gods]] as demons in rags. A player cleric of Aurel travelling with thieves and wizards will have a great deal
to report and is watched by the Church to see whether they do. Inquisitors are drawn from his clergy.

Aurel's miracles come at noon most readily. Some priests claim their prayers fail after dark; the Church says this is
a lack of faith in the priest.

Hooks:
- A merchant in Hask swore an oath at dusk, not noon, and now argues in a Sunward court that it does not bind him. The
  court wants witnesses to the exact hour; the party were there.
- A Sunward priest's prayers have begun to fail at noon as well. He suspects the god has turned from him for an old
  shrine-burning, and wants someone to rebuild the shrine quietly.
- [[Magdal Crane]] pays well for proof that a guildmaster leaves bread for [[Old Tallow]].
