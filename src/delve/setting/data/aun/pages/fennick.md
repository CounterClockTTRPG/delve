Fennick is the god of the coin on its edge: the moment before it falls. Merchants pray to him for a good bargain,
thieves for a good night, gamblers for a good throw, and travellers for a road without bandits, often to the same
priest in the same morning. He is the most popular of the [[Great Gods]] in [[Hask]], where trade and theft are
neighbours, and his temple in [[Templegate]] has a counting-house on one side and, people say, a fence's back door on
the other.

## What his people do

- **The spin.** Before a deal, both sides spin a coin on the table and let it fall. Nobody pays attention to which way
  it falls; the spin is the prayer. A merchant who won't spin is thought to be planning to cheat.
- **The sill coin.** A coin balanced on its edge on a windowsill asks Fennick to watch the house. Thieves who find one
  take it as a fee and leave the rest. Mostly.
- **The Toss.** On 13 Fennick, in high summer, debts can be settled by a single coin toss before a priest: double or
  nothing. Some fortunes in Hask date from that day; so do some suicides.
- **Taboos.** Refusing a fair wager from a friend; cheating a fellow traveller on the road; stealing from a priest of
  Fennick (it is allowed, but you must leave something of equal worth).
- **Sayings.** "Edge up." "Fennick has both faces." Adventurers about to go into the [[Undercroft]] touch a
  [[Ferryman's Toe]] and say "Let it land soft."

## His clerics

A cleric of Fennick is a broker: they arrange meetings, carry messages, witness deals, and take a small cut. They are
good with locks, better with people, and usually know which inn has rooms and which guard takes coin. Fennick's priests
are the ones who bargain openly with [[Small Gods]], and they act as go-betweens when a guild or village needs a
deal struck with one. In a party a Fennickite is lucky, cheerful, and inclined to wager the party's share of the loot
on a game of bones.

::: dm
Fennick's temple in Hask and the [[Quiet Hand]] have an old arrangement: the temple witnesses the Hand's agreements
between its thieves and keeps the copies, and the Hand never robs the temple's donors. Neither will admit it. The
temple's chief priest is one of the very few who has met [[Widow|the Widow]], and could not tell you her face.

Hooks:
- A merchant lost his house on the Toss and swears the priest's coin was weighted. If he is right, a dozen fortunes are
  in question.
- A small god in [[Wetside]] wants to strike a bargain with the party and asks for a Fennickite as witness. There is
  none in the party; the nearest one wants a cut.
:::
