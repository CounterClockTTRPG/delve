Corvanne sits on both banks of the [[Ambre]] some ten days' drift below [[Hask]], where the river narrows between two
limestone bluffs. It is the northernmost and the most martial of the [[Lantern Principalities]], and it lives off the
river: three great iron chains hang across the Ambre between the bluffs, and no barge passes until the chains are
lowered and the toll is paid.

## The Dark Law

Corvanne's oldest law says that anyone in the streets between sunset and sunrise must carry a lit lamp. A person
without one is "unlit", and may be killed by anyone without penalty. The law is meant to keep out thieves and
assassins, and it does neither, but it has made Corvanne's lamp-sellers rich and its nights beautiful: thousands of
small lights moving through the lanes and over the bridges of the upper town.

Visitors who arrive after dark buy a lamp at the gate. The gate lamps burn for about two hours. Locals know this.

## The city

- **The Chainhouse.** A squat fortress on the east bluff that works the chains. Its captain answers only to the prince
  and takes his own cut, which everyone knows and the prince allows.
- **The Upper Town.** Villas of the old families on terraces, each with a lamp-tower; the height of a family's tower
  is fixed by law and fought over in court.
- **The Wharves.** Below the bluffs, where barges wait for the chains. Riverfolk boatmen, Hask factors and Free Company
  recruiters drink side by side.
- **The Lantern Steps.** A stair of four hundred steps to the prince's palace. Once a year, on the first night of
  Ashen, the prince shows the House's Lantern from the top step, and the whole city puts out its lamps.

## The prince

Prince Severin is forty, a good soldier and a bad loser. He keeps a standing company of the
[[Free Companies|Red Lanterns]] on contract, the only prince who can afford to, and dreams of taking the chains
upriver to Hask itself.

::: dm
**The Lantern of Corvanne** is a Thaumarch street-lamp, beautifully made, that still lights itself at dusk. It is not
a binding-lamp. Prince Severin does not know this; his court sorcerer, a frightened man named Aubric Lisle, does, and
has been paid by House Orsolin for the knowledge.

**The Dark Law** has a use nobody admits: the prince's own men go unlit on purpose, and kill on purpose.

**Hooks.**
- A party arriving at dusk is sold short-burning lamps by the gatekeeper, who works for a gang that waits in the
  lanes for the lights to go out.
- The Chainhouse captain will let one barge through unchecked for a price. The cargo he is being asked to pass is a
  crated brass servant bound for Amaranthe, and it is not entirely still.
- Severin wants the party to measure the Ferryman's ford at Hask: depth, current, where a chain could be anchored.
  The Ferryman will hear about it.
:::
