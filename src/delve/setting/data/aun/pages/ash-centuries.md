The three hundred years after the [[Unmaking]] are called the **Ash Centuries**. Little was written down, because
few people could write and fewer had the time.

## What happened

- **Dark winters.** For a generation after the fall the sky was dim and summers were short. Grain failed across the
  north.
- **Famine and plague.** Whole provinces emptied. Plague pits are still found under the fields of [[Kingdom of Calder|Calder]]; ploughmen leave them alone and set a stone over them for [[Velis]].
- **The lost roads.** The Thaumarch roads cracked and were overgrown, bridges fell, and travel between regions nearly
  stopped. Villages that had been a day apart forgot each other's names.
- **Peoples on the move.** The Hold-folk of the [[Northern Holds]] raided south in longships each summer. The steppe
  clans of the [[Horse Steppe]] swept west to east and back. The surviving Thaumarch kin fled south and became the
  [[Lantern Principalities]].
- **The small gods grow.** With the [[Great Gods]] withdrawn and the churches broken, people prayed to what was near:
  the ford, the well, the hill. The [[Small Gods]] grew fat in these centuries.

## How it ended

Slowly. Winters eased. Villages walled themselves and then grew into towns. The [[Ferryman]] at [[Hask Ford]] kept
taking his coin, and a crossing that the Ferryman kept became a place people came back to. In 341 RY Aldric the
Shepherd-King united the western valleys as Calder, and most historians close the Ash Centuries there.

## What is left

Barrow-fields, plague stones, burned villages under the turf, hoards buried by people who never came back for them,
and songs: the Hold-folk sagas and the steppe clans' long chants both begin in these years.

::: dm
Ash Century ruins are poor in magic but rich in coin, because people buried what they had and died. They make good
low-level jobs.

- A hoard from these years is usually guarded by whatever small god the burier paid to watch it. That god has been
  waiting eight hundred years and expects the rest of its fee.
- Some plague pits were not plague. The [[Open Mouth]] began in these centuries, among the starving.
:::
