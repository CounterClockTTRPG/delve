Hask Ford is the crossing the city grew up around: the place where the old Thaumarch road runs down a broken stone
ramp into the [[Ambre]] on one bank and climbs out of it on the other. Once, the story goes, the river here was
shallow enough to wade at midsummer. Nobody has waded it in living memory. The water is a quarter-mile wide, brown,
and deeper in the middle than any boatman's pole.

## The stairs and the ferries

On the east bank the road comes down the **Toll Stair**, broad Thaumarch steps worn into dips by a thousand years of
feet, with the toll-house of the [[Ferrymen's Guild]] at the top. On the west bank it climbs the **Wet Stair** into
[[Wetside]], slimy, crowded and loud. Between them, from dawn to dusk, the licensed ferries go back and forth: flat
barges for carts and cattle, long skiffs for foot passengers, a gilded launch for guildmasters.

A crossing is one copper toe, the [[Ferryman's Toe]]. Every ferry carries a bronze bowl at the bow; the first coin of
each crossing goes over the side, for the [[Ferryman]]. Passengers who are watching closely will see the ferry-mate
mutter to the water before casting off. Passengers who aren't watching will be told to take their hats off.

## The bad teeth

Three times in the city's history someone has tried to bridge the Ford: in 855, in 940 and in 1032. Each time the
pilings went in, the first span went up, and the builders went into the river. The stumps are still there in
midstream, black and furred with weed, and the ferries steer wide of them. The worst is the **Councillor's Pier**,
the stump of 1032, where a guildmaster who had paid for the bridge was found tied to his own piling the morning after
it was blessed.

## Rules of the water

- No rope may be strung across the river, not even a fishing line.
- No boat but a licensed ferry may carry a paying passenger across.
- A swimmer must throw in a toe first. A drowned swimmer without a toe in his purse is left where he lies, as a
  warning.
- Nothing may cross on [[Ferry Night]] except the guild barges carrying the tithe.

::: dm
**What is under the water.** The Ford is the Ferryman's body, as far as a small god has one. Divers who go down at the
stumps (it can be done, on a rope, if you pay) find a riverbed paved with copper: three hundred years of toes, green
and fused into a crust. Taking even one up without paying it back is the surest way in Hask to be drowned within the
month.

**Hooks.**
- *The unpaid crossing.* A Margravate courier crossed last night by a smuggler's skiff without paying. The skiff came
  ashore empty. The courier's dispatch case is snagged on the Councillor's Pier, and three parties want it.
- *Copper divers.* A Wetside gang has found a way to take copper from the riverbed: they leave a living thing in its
  place. The ferrymen want them found before the Ferryman decides who to blame.
