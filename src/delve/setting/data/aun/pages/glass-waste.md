East of the [[Margravate]] and the [[Lantern Principalities]] the grass thins, the soil turns to grey crust, and then
the ground begins to shine. This is the Glass Waste, the heartland of the old [[Thaumarchy]], burned to glass in one
night at the [[Unmaking]] and empty of any law since. It runs some four hundred miles west to east and as many north to
south, and at its heart are the ruins of [[Ishavel]], the Lantern of the World.

## The land

The Waste is not flat. The fire that made it ran like water, and it set into dunes, ripples, frozen waves and long
glass ridges taller than a house. Some is green, some smoke-black, some clear as a window over buried fields. Between
the glass lie stretches of grey ash and sand, and here and there the brass ribs of a fallen tower, the line of a road
that once did not wear, or a field of standing figures that were once [[Brass Servants]].

## Dangers

- **Thirst.** There is no running water. The pickers know some cisterns, and the cisterns are the pickers' secret.
- **The sun.** At noon the glass throws light back up into the eyes. Travellers walk at dawn and dusk, wear veils of
  black gauze, and lie still under a cloth through the midday.
- **Glass-wind.** Storms lift sand and fine glass and drive it sideways. A glass-wind takes the skin off a hand left
  bare. Shelter is anywhere solid.
- **Cuts.** Every picker has scars on hands and feet. Boots are soled with three layers of hide and still wear
  through in a month.
- **The dead.** The people of the old cities did not all leave. Some are shapes in the glass, some walk at night,
  repeating something, and a few speak.
- **The [[Glass Saint]].** A shimmer on the horizon in the shape of a robed figure, said to lead the lost to water.
  Pickers follow it only when they have nothing left to lose.

## Who goes there

- **The [[Glass-Pickers]]**: guides and scavengers, sworn to their own oaths, working out of camp-towns on the edge.
  The largest is Shardsend, on the Waste's south-west rim, three days from [[Tessarine]].
- **The Margrave's diggers**: since 1187 a great work, the Vael Dig, has been carried on in the north-west of the
  Waste, with a thousand labourers, an orc guard of the [[Grey Legions]], and a road of iron rails laid back toward
  [[Vaelholt]]. Pickers who come too near are hanged on the rails.
- **Treasure-seekers**: crews from [[Hask]] and the Principalities, who have heard that a single Thaumarch lens or
  brass hand will buy a farm. Some of them come back.

## What can be found

Clear "lamp-glass" for lenses; black glass for knives; brass fittings, gears and plates; Thaumarch coins (small,
square, stamped with an eye); sealed jars of writing that the [[Collegium of the Lamp]] buys by the line; and, very
rarely, a thing that still works.

::: dm
**The Vael Dig.** In 1187 the diggers broke into a buried chamber under the glass and found a brass hand the size of a
barn, open, palm up, attached to something much larger still underground. The DM decides what it is: a Thaumarch
war-engine, the throne-servant of a House, or the cradle that once held one of the [[Seven Lanterns]]. The Margrave
believes it is the means to replace his failing [[Ember Crown]]. Something in the dig has started to hum at night.

**Hooks.**
- A picker crew offers to guide the party to a cistern nobody else knows, for a share of what they find past it. The
  cistern is guarded by a brass servant that has started to move.
- [[Ilse Varrow]] of the Collegium wants rubbings from a wall in a buried Ishavel library before the Margrave's men reach
  it. They are two weeks away.
- A ghost on the old road asks every traveller the same question in Thaumarch. The first party to answer it rightly
  will be shown a door.
:::
