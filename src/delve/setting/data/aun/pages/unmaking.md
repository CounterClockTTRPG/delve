The **Unmaking** is the night the years are counted from. On the night the [[Great Binding]] was to close, the gods
answered.

## The night

[[Ishavel]], the Lantern of the World, burned to glass between dusk and dawn. Survivors far away saw the eastern sky
go white, then red, then dark. The Thaumarchs died or vanished, almost all of them in that night. Across the Reaches,
every great work failed at once: towers fell where they stood, the undying roads cracked, lamps went out, and the
brass servants stopped mid-step, often mid-task. A story told in every province is of a brass servant found years
later still holding a cup out to an empty chair.

The heartland around Ishavel became the [[Glass Waste]]. The [[Seven Lanterns]] were scattered; nobody agrees how.

## After

The [[Great Gods]] withdrew. Prayer still worked, through priests, but the gods no longer walked or spoke plainly, and
they have not since. In the quiet they left, the [[Small Gods]] grew. Then came the [[Ash Centuries]].

## Why

Every church has an answer.

- The [[Sunward Church]]: [[Aurel]] judged the empire's pride.
- The grey priests of [[Velis]]: the gods did not strike; they pulled free, and the strain broke everything.
- The priests of [[Oruun]]: they dreamed it, and nobody listened.
- [[Elves]]: say nothing, which the curious find maddening.

## At the table

People still say "glass and ash" as an oath, and "since the Unmaking" for "since forever". Finding something whole
that was made before the Unmaking is called "finding a morning", and it is the dream of every crew in the
[[Undercroft]].

::: dm
Not all the Thaumarchs died. Some stepped out of the world as Ishavel burned and could not step back; a few remain,
diminished, in sealed places. One of them may be under the [[Pale Tower]], beside the Lantern that lies there.

- Not everything stopped. Under the glass, some great works still hold a charge they never spent. The brass hand the
  size of a barn that the Margravate's diggers uncovered in 1187 belongs to one of them, and it has begun to hum.
- A brass servant that wakes today resumes the task it was doing on that night, exactly.
:::
