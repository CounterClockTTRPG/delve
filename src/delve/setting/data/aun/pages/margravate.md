The Margravate was once Calder's eastern march, a border county facing the [[Glass Waste]]. In 887 its lord,
[[Teodric Vael]], opened a Thaumarch tomb at the edge of the Waste and came out wearing the [[Ember Crown]]. In 888 he
broke from [[Kingdom of Calder|Calder]] and called himself Margrave of his own land. He has not aged a day since. Three
hundred years later he still rules from [[Vaelholt]], and the Margravate is the best-run, best-armed and most
frightened country in the Reaches.

## The land

Iron hills and ash-grey heath, cut by fast streams and mine-workings. In the north the eastern [[Hornwall]], held by
orc tribes who serve the Margrave. In the west the old border with Calder, running down to [[Redmere]]. In the east the
land turns to scrub, then to cracked earth, then to glass. Forges burn everywhere, and the rain comes down grey.

## Rule

The Margrave rules alone. Below him are his **reeves**, one to each of the twelve districts, chosen for skill and
removed for failure, often permanently. There are no barons; Vael hanged the last of them in the 890s. The army is the
[[Grey Legions]]; the spies and tutors are the [[Ember Court]]. Law is simple, written and enforced to the letter.

## Law and custom

- **The tally.** Every subject over twelve carries an iron tally stamped with name, district and work. To be found
  without one is to be hanged. Foreigners buy a brass tally at the border for a silver a week, and should not lose it.
- **The roads are safe.** Bandits do not last. The Margravate's roads are lined with gallows, which the locals call
  "the Margrave's orchards". A purse dropped on the road will be there next week, and so will the man who picked it up.
- **Taxes in kind.** A tenth of everything: grain, iron, sons. Every family sends one child in four to the Legions,
  the mines or the diggings.
- **Gods.** Temples are allowed if their priests pray for the Margrave by name. The [[Sunward Church]] is banned. Small
  gods are left alone if they are useful; a mine-god who warns of bad air gets a reeve's offering every month.
- **Prices.** Iron is cheap: a good sword costs half what it does in [[Hask]]. Wine is dear, and salt dearer.

## Neighbours

Calder and the [[Sunward Theocracy]] fought the Margravate for seven years (1104–1111) and did not win; the
[[Seven Years' War]] ended in a stalemate after Redmere. The dwarves of [[Deephorn]] hate the Margrave for the eastern
mines he took from them. [[Hask]] sells him everything he cannot make, and he lets it stay free because it is more
useful that way. In 1187 the Grey Legions mustered on the Calder march, then sat there, waiting.

## The diggings

Since 1187 the Margrave's diggers have been working deep in the Glass Waste, with an army camp to guard them. Locals
say they have found "something large". Wagons go east full of timber, rope and convicts, and come back with nothing
anyone is allowed to see.

## At the table

Ordinary people in the Margravate are not wicked; they are tired, careful and safer than most. That is the horror of
it. Adventurers here forge tallies, smuggle people out, guide reeves to the Waste, or are hired by the Margrave
himself, who pays on time and in full.

::: dm
**The truth.** The Ember Crown holds the trapped spark of a lesser power and it is burning out; Vael knows. The dig in
the Waste is his search for a replacement, a stronger fire, and the thing they have found may be a
[[Seven Lanterns|Lantern]] or worse. The muster on the Calder march is a screen to keep eyes west.

**Hooks.**
- A reeve offers the party honest work: escort a convoy to the diggings. The convoy is forty convicts. Half will not
  come back, and the reeve does not intend that the escort should either.
- A family in a border village wants their son brought back from the Legions before he is posted east.
- A forged-tally maker in Vaelholt is selling a list of everyone he has ever served. Several buyers want it.
:::
