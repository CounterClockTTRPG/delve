Templegate is the hill north of [[Tollside]], on [[Hask]]'s east bank, where the twelve great gods keep house. The
temples were built over four centuries by whichever guild had money to spend on its soul, and they jostle for space
like merchants at a fair. Pilgrims come up the Toll Stair, buy a candle at the bottom of the hill and a blessing at
the top, and are robbed somewhere in between.

## The temples

- **The Sun House** of [[Aurel]]: the newest, whitest and largest, finished only thirty years ago with money from
  [[Candor]]. It has a golden scale on its dome that catches the sunrise and blinds the ferrymen. Attached to it is
  the **House of the Scale**, the Sunward mission, under the inquisitor [[Magdal Crane]].
- **The Grey House** of [[Velis]], the oldest stones on the hill, with the city's boneyard behind it. Velis's priests
  bury anyone, including hanged men from Gallows Hill and drowned men the river gives back.
- **The Red Hall** of [[Karsk]], half temple and half hiring-hall, where the [[Free Companies]] post their muster
  rolls.
- **Fennick's Counter**, the temple of [[Fennick]], where merchants and thieves pray at the same rail and the
  offering bowl is chained down.
- **The Forge** of [[Asheth]], kept by the Smiths' guild, and the plain shrine of [[Hollin]] beside it.
- Smaller houses of [[Marrah]], [[Sere]], [[Isaine]], [[Vorm]] and [[Oruun]]. There is no temple to [[Nhul]]. There
  is a gap in the row where one stood before the city was a city; nobody builds on it.

## Small gods on the hill

At the foot of the hill, by the Toll Stair, is a crooked little niche with a stone foot in it, smooth as soap: the
shrine of the [[Ferryman]], older than any temple above it. Haskers touch it on their way up. The Sunward priests
have asked the Council three times to remove it. The Council has said no three times, and fined them for asking.

## Getting help

Templegate is where you go for healing, blessing or burial. None of it is cheap. A priest's prayer for a wound costs
what a ferryman earns in a month; a true miracle, if the god grants one, costs more and is talked about for years.
Most crews make do with Velis's priests, who charge less because their god gets everyone in the end.
