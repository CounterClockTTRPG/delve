Calder is the old kingdom of the western valleys: green downs cut by chalk roads, sheep by the hundred thousand,
castles on every hill worth holding and a good many that are not. It was made in 341 RY, when **Aldric the
Shepherd-King** talked, bribed and fought the valley lords into one crown, and it has been proud of itself ever since.
The pride has lasted better than the money.

Its capital is [[Aldmoor]]. Its king is [[Bertil III]], seventy-one years old and without an heir. Everyone in the
kingdom, from the barons down to the shepherds, is waiting to see what happens when he dies.

## The land

- **The downs.** Rolling chalk hills in the kingdom's heart, bare on top, wooded in the folds. Barrows line the ridges;
  shepherds will not sleep on them. One of them is the [[Hill That Listens]]. [[Gnomes]] live in the downs in burrow
  villages, cutting gems and mending pots, and keep on good terms with every small god for twenty miles.
- **The western marches.** Calder ends where the trees thin into the grass of the [[Horse Steppe]]. Border keeps
  watch the plains; trade with [[Ulagh Khan]]'s clans runs both ways, and so do the raids.
- **The north.** Open moor runs up to the Iron Sea between the steppe and the western end of the [[Hornwall]].
  Longships from the [[Northern Holds]] come ashore here in raiding years.
- **The east.** Past Aldmoor lies the [[Thornwold]], which no Calderish king has ever owned, and beyond it [[Hask]]. A
  strip of Calderish land runs north of the forest and across the upper [[Ambre]] to [[Redmere]]: the **march**, all
  that is left of the kingdom's east since [[Teodric Vael]] took the rest in 888 and made it the [[Margravate]].

## Rule

The king rules through his barons, and the barons rule through whoever they can afford. There are twenty-odd great
lords and some three hundred knights holding land of them. Each baron keeps his own court, his own feuds and his own
small army; the king keeps the Crown Council, which meets in Aldmoor when he calls it and argues until he dismisses it.

The crown is poor. It sold [[Free City of Hask|Hask]] its freedom in 901 to pay its debts (the [[Bought Freedom]]);
it spent what was left on the [[Seven Years' War]]. Calder now borrows from House Orlemont in Hask, and the barons know
exactly how much.

## Life and custom

- **Wool is everything.** The great fairs at Aldmoor and the valley towns sell fleece to Hask's merchants. A baron's
  wealth is counted in flocks; a bride's dowry is counted in sheep.
- **The crook.** Every king of Calder is crowned holding Aldric's shepherd's crook, not a sceptre. Calderish lords
  carry a little silver crook on their seal, and swear by it.
- **Faith.** Calder prays to [[Hollin]] for the harvest, to [[Velis]] for the dead, to [[Karsk]] before a fight. The
  [[Sunward Church]] has built new temples in the towns since the war and is popular with barons who want the
  [[Sunward Theocracy]] as a friend. The village shrines to small gods have not gone anywhere.
- **Prices.** A fat sheep, 3 silver; a night at a valley inn, 2 copper; a knight's warhorse, 200 gold, more if it has
  been to the steppe and back.

## At the table

The [[Sunward Theocracy]] to the south is an uncomfortable ally; the [[Margravate]] is the old enemy, and its Grey
Legions have sat on the march since 1187.

Calder is a good place to start a campaign that wants castles, old barrows, sheep thieves, border keeps and
succession politics. Adventurers here are hired swords, and knights who lost their horses in the summer tournaments end up in the
[[Free Companies]] or the [[Undercroft]]. A baron needs a rival's letters stolen, a barrow emptied of
whatever is walking out of it, or a steppe raider brought back alive.

::: dm
**The succession.** Three barons are already buying swords: **Wystan Morrow**, Baron of the Ridings, a cousin of the
king through his grandmother and the best claim on paper; **Adela Fenwright**, Countess of Lowmere, the richest lord in
the kingdom, whose claim is weak and whose purse is not; and **Hamo Strake**, Lord of the March, who holds the eastern
keeps against the Margrave and has the only real army. The Sunward Church backs Morrow. The [[Ember Court]] is paying
someone close to Strake.

**Hooks.**
- A baron hires the party to find the king's rumoured hidden heir, said to be a shepherd boy in the downs, before
  the other two do (see [[Bertil III]]). Each of them wants a different result.
- Barrow-robbers in the downs have opened a mound and something with a bronze face is walking the chalk roads at night.
  It may be a [[Brass Servants|brass servant]], awake.
- House Orlemont wants a debt collected from a baron who has decided not to pay it. The baron has a castle.
:::
