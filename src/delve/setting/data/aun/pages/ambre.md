The Ambre is the great river of the Reaches. It rises in the snowfields of the [[Hornwall]], comes down cold and fast
past the dwarf-roads, runs south between the [[Thornwold]] and the open country of the east, and slows into a wide
brown water by the time it reaches [[Hask]]. From there it winds south through the vineyards of the
[[Lantern Principalities]], past [[Corvanne]]'s chains, and spreads into a delta of reeds and channels at
[[Amaranthe]] before it meets the Sunder Sea. It is some six hundred miles long, and everything worth carrying in the
middle of the Reaches goes up or down it.

## The river's gods

The Ambre is not one god but many. Every ford, weir, pool and bend of any size has its own, and the boatmen know them
the way a carter knows the inns.

- **The [[Lady of the Weir]]**, at a weir a few miles above Hask, takes flowers, ribbons and the occasional drowned
  miller. She is jealous of the Ferryman and sends snags to his ferries.
- **The [[Ferryman]]** of [[Hask Ford]] is the oldest and strongest. He takes a coin from every crossing and drowns
  anyone who builds a bridge. Because of him, the Hask ferries are the only way over the Ambre for a hundred miles in
  either direction.
- Below Hask the river gods are smaller and more numerous: a catfish god at Sallen Bend who wants bread, the
  Two Sisters (a pair of rocks who are said to quarrel), and a hundred nameless pools where a boatman drops a crust
  without thinking about it.

## Who lives on it

The **Riverfolk**, halfling boat-families, own most of the barges and know every shoal. They work the Hask ferries,
run the mills, and smuggle whatever pays. Above Hask the river passes the leaning [[Pale Tower]]; the bargemen do not
moor in its shadow. Below, the Lantern towns tax every mile, and Corvanne's chains stop everything until the toll is
paid.

## Travel

- Downstream from Hask to Amaranthe: about three weeks by barge, with stops for tolls, longer in a dry summer.
- Upstream: twice as long, by sail and tow-horse, and the tow-paths belong to whoever holds the bank.
- A Riverfolk pilot costs a silver piece a day and is worth it; the Ambre moves its sandbars every spring.
- Spring floods come in Marrah. A barge caught by the flood does not stop until something stops it.

**Secrets of the river.**
- Thaumarch roads ran under the Ambre in tunnels, not over it. At least one tunnel, below Hask, is dry and
  forgotten. The Quiet Hand would pay a fortune for it; the Ferryman would consider it a bridge.
- The Lady of the Weir has made an offer to the Riverfolk: drown the Ferryman's tithe on Ferry Night, and she will
  give them a ford of their own.

**Hooks.**
- A barge-family's eldest daughter has gone missing at Sallen Bend; the catfish god has had no bread for a month,
  since the family turned Sunward.
- A Corvanne agent is sounding the depths at Hask Ford at night, by lamp. The ferry boatmen want him found before the
  Ferryman finds him.
- A sunk barge in the Lantern reach holds a Collegium scholar's whole library in sealed jars.
