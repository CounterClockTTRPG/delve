South of [[Hask]] the [[Ambre]] slows, widens and runs brown between terraced vineyards, walled towns and villas
with too many lamps. These are the Lantern Principalities: seven small states ruled by seven princely Houses, each
descended (or claiming to be) from one of the Seven Houses of the [[Thaumarchy]]. Their people, the Lanterners, are
olive-skinned, dark-haired and pale-eyed, and will tell you about their blood before they tell you their name.

## How they came to be

When [[Ishavel]] burned at the [[Unmaking]], the surviving kin of the Thaumarchs fled south through the
[[Ash Centuries]] and founded the Lantern Kingdom on the lower Ambre. It held for some four hundred years. In the
730s the royal line failed, seven cousins each proved themselves the true heir, and the kingdom broke apart in the
[[Heirs' War]]. It has never been put back together, and the seven Houses would sooner poison one another than
try.

## The Houses

Three Houses matter. [[Corvanne]] holds the river and its chains, upstream nearest Hask. [[Tessarine]] holds the dry
eastern hills on the edge of the [[Glass Waste]], and the glass trade. [[Amaranthe]] holds the Ambre's mouth and the
sea. The other four, Sabrenne, Dulcamare, Orsolin and Quillane, are small, proud and poor, and survive by marrying
their daughters to the great three and selling their votes in every quarrel.

Every prince claims to hold one of the [[Seven Lanterns]], the binding-lamps of the old Houses. Each shows it at
great feasts, behind glass, at a distance. No two princes accept each other's Lantern as genuine.

## Life and custom

- **Ceremony.** A Lantern court can spend an afternoon deciding who sits nearer the window. Titles are long; using the
  short form to a noble's face is an insult, and duels are fought over it.
- **Poison.** It is an art here, like music. Wealthy houses keep a taster; the very wealthy keep two, so that one can
  watch the other. Antidotes are sold openly in the markets. Most of them are chalk.
- **Odd laws.** Each city has its own. In Corvanne you must carry a light after dark or anyone may kill you; in
  Tessarine the host tastes every dish first; in Amaranthe a dead man's debts pass to whoever sits in his chair at the
  funeral. Travellers are expected to know, and the law does not forgive them for not knowing.
- **Magic.** Each prince keeps a court sorcerer if one can be found, as a sign of the old blood. These are rarely
  better than other wizards and often worse, but they are better dressed. The [[Sunward Church]] hates them for it.
- **Money.** The silver coin is the *wick*, stamped with a flame and the minting House's badge. A Corvanne wick
  is good anywhere; a Quillane wick is worth what the moneychanger says.

## Neighbours

The [[Sunward Theocracy]] to the west calls the Principalities a nest of sorcerers and would like an excuse. The
[[Ember Court]] places tutors in princely households and has for two centuries. Hask's House Velloure carries their
silk and southern spice north, and the princes hire the [[Free Companies]] for every war, which is often, and pay them
late, which is always.

## For the table

The river towns pay well for crews to clear Thaumarch cellars under old villas. The cellars are the reason the villas
were built there.

::: dm
**What the Houses want.** All seven want the same thing: to be the one who reunites the Lantern Kingdom. Each believes
a genuine Lantern would make it so. Five of the seven they display are certainly not binding-lamps: four are fine
Thaumarch street-lamps and one is coloured glass and a candle. The other two are kept shut and have not been opened in
living memory (see [[Tessarine]]); if the campaign wants a second true Lantern above ground, it is one of those.

**Hooks.**
- A minor House (Orsolin) hires the party to steal the Lantern of Corvanne during the yearly showing, not to keep it
  but to prove publicly it is false. Corvanne will know within a week who did it.
- An Ember Court tutor in Amaranthe has raised the prince's heir to admire the Margrave. The boy is twelve and asks
  the party, quite sincerely, how one goes about living forever.
- The Sunward Church is paying Free Company captains in the Principalities not to take Lantern contracts this year.
  Someone wants the princes without soldiers.
:::
