Hollin is the peasant's god. He has no great temple worth the name in any city; his shrine in [[Templegate]] is a
barn with a painted door. But every village in the [[Kingdom of Calder]] and most of the Reaches keeps a sheaf bound
in red over its shrine, and every hearth has a corner where a crust is left for him. He asks for little, gives a little,
and is thought to be the one great god who still notices ordinary people.

## What his people do

- **The crust.** The heel of every loaf goes on the hearth-stone overnight. In the morning it goes to the chickens,
  never back to the table.
- **Harvest Home.** On the last day of Hollin the last sheaf of the harvest is bound in red cloth and carried round the
  village, and the feast is held in the biggest barn. The first loaf of the new grain goes to Hollin; the second goes
  to whatever small god the village keeps, and nobody pretends otherwise. See [[Harvest Home]].
- **Guest-bread.** Anyone who asks at a door for bread is given it. A household that refuses is thought to have
  insulted the god, and its next harvest is watched with interest by the neighbours.
- **Taboos.** Burning a standing field, even an enemy's; letting a hearth go cold in winter on purpose; selling the
  last sack of seed corn. Soldiers who burn crops find that village priests of Hollin will not bless them, feed them
  or bury their friends.
- **Sayings.** "Bread first, then words." "Hollin keeps a small house." A thing done well and modestly is "done
  Hollin's way."

## His clerics

Hollin's priests are humble because they are poor. A village priest of Hollin is usually a farmer's younger son or
daughter who learned the rites from the last one. They bless fields, set bones, settle boundary quarrels, and, quietly,
keep the peace between the village and its [[Small Gods]]: they know which well wants a ribbon and which hill wants
silence. A cleric of Hollin on the road is a good cook, a decent healer, and stubborn about sharing food with anyone
who looks hungry, prisoners included.
