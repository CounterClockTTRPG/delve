**Harvest Home** is the feast of [[Hollin]], Lord of Sheaves, held on the last day of his month, 30 Hollin, when the
grain is in. It is the peasant's great holiday, and the one that matters most in the countryside.

## The feast

- **The last sheaf.** The last sheaf cut in each village is bound in red cord, carried in at the head of a procession
  and hung over the door of the barn until next year. Whoever cut it is "the Sheaf" for the night: they eat first,
  drink free, and may not be refused a dance.
- **The first loaf.** The first loaf baked from the new flour goes to the [[Small Gods]]: the well, the ford, the
  hill, the millrace. Hollin's priests do not object; many bake the loaf themselves. Only the second loaf goes to
  Hollin's shrine.
- **Long tables.** Everyone eats together, lord and labourer, at tables set in the fields. Quarrels are forbidden at
  the table; they are settled the next day, usually with fists.
- **Hiring.** Labourers who want new masters stand in the square at noon with a straw in their hats.

## Around the Reaches

- In the [[Kingdom of Calder]] it is the greatest feast of the year, and barons are judged by the beer they give.
- In the [[Sunward Theocracy]] the first loaf goes to [[Aurel]]'s temple, and leaving one out for a small god is a
  fine, or worse.
- In [[Hask]] the guild of bakers holds a parade through [[Tollside]], and loaves are thrown from the boats on the
  [[Ambre]] for the [[Ferryman]] and the [[Lady of the Weir]], carefully the same size.
- Among the [[Halflings|Riverfolk]] the feast lasts three days.

## At the table

Harvest Home is the best day of the year to be a stranger in a village: food for everyone, nobody asking questions.
It is the worst day to need a villager to do anything.

::: dm
The first loaf is a small god's due. Where it is withheld, the god goes hungry for a year and remembers who refused it.

- A village in Calder where the Sunward Church forbade the first loaf last year has had a bad year since: wells
  souring, children sleepwalking. The villagers want a crew to make peace with their god without the priest finding out.
- The Ferryman and the Lady of the Weir count the loaves. If one gets more, there is trouble on the river for a month.
:::
