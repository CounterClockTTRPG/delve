The [[Northern Holds]] have no king, and the Hold-folk like it that way. What they have instead is the Moot: the
yearly gathering of the jarls at [[Skarnholm]], where feuds are judged, blood-prices set, marriages arranged, raids
planned and the law of the Holds spoken aloud so that nobody can claim to have forgotten it.

## When and where

The Moot meets for nine days in Ashen, when the fjord ice breaks and the longships can come in. It sits on the
Moot-field above Skarnholm harbour, round a great flat stone called the Lawstone. Every jarl who comes brings a ship
and a following; in a good year there are sixty ships in the harbour and more people on the field than live in the
town.

## How it works

- **The truce.** From the first day to the last, no blood may be shed within a day's sail of Skarnholm. A man who
  breaks the Moot-truce is outlawed by every hold and may be killed by anyone, anywhere, for the rest of his life.
- **The Lawspeaker.** A skald chosen for life, who speaks a third of the law of the Holds aloud each year from
  memory, so that the whole is heard every three years. If she misremembers, the law changes. Bribing a Lawspeaker is
  the worst crime in the Holds, and the most often attempted.
- **Speaking first.** The jarl who speaks first sets what the Moot will talk about. By custom it is the jarl of
  Skarnholm: now [[Sigrun Halfhand]], who uses it hard.
- **Judgement.** Any free man or woman may bring a case. The jarls judge by shouting, and the Lawspeaker says which
  shout was loudest. A case lost can be appealed by duel.
- **Blood-price.** Every killing has a price in silver, set by the rank of the dead. Paying it ends a feud. Refusing to
  accept it is allowed, but loses the family the Moot's protection.

## Raids

The Moot decides, loosely, which coasts the Holds will raid that summer and which they will trade with. A jarl who
raids a coast the Moot has put under peace pays blood-price for every death. A jarl who raids an unnamed coast pays
nothing.

## For the table

Outsiders may come to the Moot under the truce, to trade, to sue, or to hire Hold-folk. A foreigner who brings a case
needs a jarl to speak for them, and jarls' voices are bought with gifts.
