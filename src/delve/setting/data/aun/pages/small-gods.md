A **small god** belongs to one place: a ford, a hill, a well, a sewer, a glacier, one street. On its own ground it can
be very strong; a mile away it is weak; a few days' walk off, it is nothing. Small gods grow fat on offerings and
starve when forgotten. They can be bargained with, bribed, flattered, tricked and, rarely, killed.

Most people deal with a small god every day and a great god a few times a year. A miller leaves a cup of flour at the
millrace. A carter taps the milestone three times. A child is told never to whistle near the old well, and doesn't.

## Where they came from

After the [[Unmaking]] the [[Great Gods]] drew back, and in the hollow they left the small gods grew. Scholars argue
whether they were always there and only louder now, or whether the gods' absence bred them like mushrooms in a cellar.
Peasants do not care.

## Known small gods

- The [[Ferryman]] of [[Hask Ford]], older than [[Hask]]; takes a coin from every crossing and drowns bridge-builders.
- [[Mother Gutter]] of Hask's sewers, friend of beggars and lost children.
- [[Old Tallow]] of [[Tallow Lane]], fat and greedy and kind.
- The [[Lady of the Weir]], above Hask, jealous of the Ferryman.
- The [[Hill That Listens]] in Calder's downs, which answers one question a year for a secret.
- The [[Drowned Bell]] off the [[Drowned Coast]], which rings before wrecks.
- The [[Glass Saint]] of the [[Glass Waste]], a mirage that leads the lost.
- [[Grandmother Frost]], the glacier that keeps the Hold-folk's dead.

## Who thinks what

- The [[Sunward Church]] calls them demons and burns their shrines. [[Anselm IX]] preaches against them every week.
- [[Hollin]]'s and [[Velis]]'s priests tolerate them, and quietly share the bread.
- [[Fennick]]'s people bargain with them as with anyone else.
- [[Gnomes]] are on easy terms with them; [[Elves]] say nothing at all.

## Rules of thumb

- A small god wants something particular: a coin, a song, a secret, a toe. Offer the wrong thing and it is an insult.
- A promise made to a small god on its ground binds. Break it and the ground remembers.
- A small god's priest has real gifts at home and none abroad.

::: dm
Use small gods as the texture of every location. Each village, crossroads or dungeon level can have one: give it a
want, a price and a grudge.

- The Sunward Church is paying crews to burn shrines along the Calder road. The small gods of those places are
  starving and desperate; one may offer the PCs anything for protection.
- A small god that is killed leaves its place empty, and something worse may move in. The [[Open Mouth]] feeds where
  small gods have died.
- Small gods are what the Thaumarchs never thought to bind. A clever PC may realise a small god can do what a great
  god now will not.
:::
