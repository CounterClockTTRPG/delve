Gnomes are the hill-folk of the downs of the [[Kingdom of Calder]]: small, brown, bright-eyed, living in burrow-houses
dug into chalk hills, with round doors painted blue. They are tinkers, gem-cutters and menders, and they travel the
roads of Calder and beyond with handcarts full of tools, fixing pots, sharpening knives and selling cut stones of
surprising quality. Every village knows the gnome who comes through in spring.

They are on friendly terms with [[Small Gods]], more than any other people, and it shapes everything about them.

## What they are like

- **Neighbourly with the unseen.** Every gnome family knows the small gods of its hill, its spring and its road, by
  name, and talks to them as you would to a neighbour over a fence. They bargain cheerfully and keep their word.
- **Clever with their hands.** They cut gems, repair clocks (the few there are), make locks and pick them. A gnome can
  mend almost anything that was made, and is curious about anything that wasn't.
- **Gossips.** Gnomes trade news as currency. A gnome tinker will give a better price for a good story, and will pass
  it on, improved.
- **Unhurried.** They live three or four human lifetimes, and their feuds, unlike dwarves', are mostly about recipes.

## Customs

- A new burrow is not dug until the hill's small god has agreed. The agreement is usually a song, a gift and a promise
  to keep the grass short.
- Gnomes keep the path to the [[Hill That Listens]], cut its chalk ear fresh each spring, and are paid in secrets.
- A gnome tinker leaves every village with one thing mended that nobody asked them to mend. It is for luck.
- Saying: "Ask the hill," meaning "ask the right person," and sometimes literally.

## At the table

A gnome character is welcome in Calder's villages and suspect in its castles. They know the small gods of a place
quickly, and can often tell who is fed and who is starving, who is angry and who can be bargained with. The
[[Sunward Church]] regards them as demon-friends, and in the [[Sunward Theocracy]] gnome tinkers have been burned with
their carts. They tend not to forgive this.

::: dm
The gnomes of the downs have quietly started hiding small gods. When a Sunward preacher comes to burn a shrine, the
gnomes coax the god (a spring-spirit, a stone-spirit) into a carried thing, a pebble or a jar of water, and take it
up into the downs. It is a dangerous trick: a god carried away from home grows weak and strange.

Hooks:
- A gnome tinker asks the party to carry a stoppered jar to a village in Calder and pour it into the new well there.
  The jar is warm, and occasionally speaks.
- A Sunward inquisitor has arrested a gnome gem-cutter in Hask. The gnome knows where three hidden small gods are kept.
:::
