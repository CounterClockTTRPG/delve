The Ferryman is the small god of [[Hask Ford]], and he was there before [[Hask]]. Before the Thaumarchs laid their road
to the water, before the Ford was a ford, something in that stretch of the [[Ambre]] took a price from everyone who
crossed: a coin, or a toe. The custom has been the coin for as long as anyone remembers. Nobody wants to find out
whether the other still stands.

He is the most honoured god in Hask and the most practical. Haskers swear by Aurel in court, marry under Isaine and
are buried by Velis, but they pay the Ferryman every time they cross the river, and they mean it.

## What he wants

- **A coin for every crossing.** The first copper toe of every ferry goes over the side. Swimmers throw one in before
  they wade. A crossing without the coin is a debt, and the Ferryman collects his debts.
- **No bridge.** Ever. Three times bridges have been begun at the Ford, in 855, 940 and 1032, and each time the
  builders drowned, the spans fell and the river rose. Hask's law against bridges is the most strictly kept law it
  has.
- **The tithe.** Once a year, on [[Ferry Night]], the [[Ferrymen's Guild]] and the other guilds row out to midstream
  by lantern-light and pour the city's tithe into the water: a tenth of a tenth of the year's tolls, in copper toes and
  never silver, while every lantern in the city burns on the banks.

## What he gives

Ferries that don't sink. A river that floods but never takes the city. The drowned given back, more or less whole, on
the third day, so Velis can bury them. And a little luck to anyone who carries his coin: the [[Ferryman's Toe]] is
the most common charm in the Reaches.

He doesn't talk. He doesn't show himself. Ferrymen say that when he's displeased the water at the Ford turns to the
colour of an old bruise and the ferry-ropes creak with no wind. When he is very displeased, someone doesn't reach the
other bank.

::: dm
The Ferryman is strong (for a small god, very strong: four centuries of copper and a whole city's attention) and
utterly bound to the Ford. A mile up or down the river he is nothing. He is not cruel, only exact. He keeps a tally
of every unpaid crossing and settles it, sooner or later, with interest. He is also jealous: the [[Lady of the Weir]]
upriver is his rival, and he hates the [[Sunward Church]], which would burn his shrine if it could.

**Hooks.**
- The tithe for Ferry Night is in the Ferrymen's vault. Someone in the guild has been skimming it for years, and this
  year it will be short. The guild needs it found, quietly, before the night.
- A player who crosses without paying (or is made to, by a thief who lifts the coin) wakes with wet feet every
  morning, and the water in their boots is rising.
:::
