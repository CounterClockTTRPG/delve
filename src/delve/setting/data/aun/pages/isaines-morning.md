**Isaine's Morning**, on 1 Isaine, is the spring festival of [[Isaine]], the Bright Singer. It opens the month and,
for most people, the real year: the winter is over and the roads are open.

## The day

- **The first song.** At dawn, everyone who can sing goes out of doors and sings the first song they think of. It is
  meant to be heard by the first lark. A bad singer is told it was their song that brought the frost.
- **Courtship.** It is the day to court. A sprig of blackthorn blossom pinned to a sleeve means "ask me"; a sprig
  given back means no, kindly. Weddings fixed on Isaine's Morning are held before the end of Marrah.
- **Players and poets.** Travelling players open their season, and every town square has a stage. In [[Hask]] the
  guilds sponsor a contest of songs in [[Templegate]], and the winner is excused tolls for a year.
- **Debts forgiven, a little.** By old custom a creditor forgives a tenth of any debt owed by someone who sings to
  them on the day. Moneylenders lock their doors.

## Around the Reaches

- In the [[Kingdom of Calder]] the shepherds bring the first lambs into the villages to be blessed.
- In the [[Sunward Theocracy]] the festival is permitted but sober: hymns to [[Aurel]], not love songs.
- In the [[Lantern Principalities]] the courts hold masked balls where marriages, and murders, are arranged.
- In the [[Northern Holds]] the ice is still on the fjords; the Hold-folk keep the day a month late and call it "the
  southerners' spring".

## At the table

A campaign begins on Isaine's Morning, 1 Isaine 1188 RY. It is a good day for strangers to meet: crowds, music,
loosened tongues and plenty of drink. Crews are formed at the [[Drowned Toe]] on Isaine's Morning more than on any
other day, because it is held to be lucky.

::: dm
Start the campaign in the crowd. A festival is cover for everything.

- In the song contest at Templegate, one entrant sings an old Thaumarch tune she does not understand; the [[Collegium of the Lamp]] notices, and so does [[Magdal Crane]].
- A blackthorn sprig pinned to a PC's sleeve by a stranger is not courtship: it is a [[Quiet Hand]] mark, and the

- A Lantern prince's envoy in Hask uses the festival to meet an [[Ember Court]] agent at the Toe.
