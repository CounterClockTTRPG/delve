In 901 RY the town of [[Hask]] bought its charter from a bankrupt king of [[Kingdom of Calder|Calder]], and has been
a free city ever since. Haskers call it **the Bought Freedom**, and say it with pride: other cities had to fight for
their liberty, and lost; Hask paid for it, and kept it.

## Before

Hask grew up around [[Hask Ford]] from about 812 RY, a toll crossing of the [[Ambre]] on the old Thaumarch road. It
paid taxes to Calder, which owned the west bank by right and the east bank by habit. The town got rich on the
crossing, because there was no other for a hundred miles and the [[Ferryman]] would not allow a bridge.

## The sale

By 900 the Calderish crown was deep in debt to the river merchants, much of it to the merchants of Hask itself. The
king of the day, needing money for a war with the steppe clans, was offered a sum in gold by the town's guilds: the
debt forgiven, plus enough to pay his army for a year, in return for Hask's charter forever. He signed.

The charter made Hask a free city under a council of its guilds, owing no lord anything but a yearly gift of a silver
toe to the King of Calder "in memory of friendship". The gift is still sent. The war, as it happens, was lost.

## The charter

The original charter hangs in the Council Hall in [[Tollside]], in a glass case watched by two guards. The nine guilds
of the **Ford Council** each keep a copy. The [[Ferrymen's Guild]], richest of them, keeps the oldest.

## Customs

- On the anniversary, 9 Ashen, Hask's guildmasters walk the bounds of the city in their chains and each pays a copper
  at the Ford.
- A Hasker insulted by a Calderish noble may say "we bought you once"; it usually leads to a fight.

::: dm
The charter has a clause no one reads aloud: it is void if the King of Calder's line fails. [[Bertil III]] has no
heir. Several Calderish barons know this and are counting the days; so is [[Benedek Sallow]], who has lawyers looking
for a way round it.

- Hiring the PCs to steal, forge or protect a guild's copy of the charter is a strong opening job.
- The silver toe sent each year to Aldmoor has been kept in the royal treasury; there are 287 of them, and a baron
  who held them all could make a pointed show of it.
:::
