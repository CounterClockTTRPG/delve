The Free City of [[Hask]] is a city that owns itself, and it has the receipt. In 901 RY the guilds of Hask Ford
bought their charter from a Calderish king who had run out of money (the [[Bought Freedom]]), and no one has ruled
them since. The realm is
the city, its two banks of the [[Ambre]], and a strip of river villages a day's ride up and down the water. It has no
army worth the name, no king and no bridge, and it is the richest town in the Reaches.

## Government

Hask is run by the **Ford Council**: nine guildmasters, one for each of the nine great guilds. The
[[Ferrymen's Guild]] sits first and speaks first; after it come the Chandlers, the Tanners and Fullers, the Smiths,
the Drapers, the Brewers, the Ropers, the Spicers and the Moneyers. The great merchant houses (Tesk, Orlemont and
Velloure) hold no seat, but each owns at least two guildmasters, and everyone knows which.

Every seven years the Council elects a **Lord Warden** to run the city between its meetings. The present Warden is
[[Benedek Sallow]], fat, clever, famously corrupt, and in his fourteenth year of office. His third election falls due
this autumn, on [[Ferry Night]], and the guilds are already quarrelling about it in the street.

Below the Council come the toll-clerks and the **Watch**: four hundred men and women in brown coats, paid late and
bribed early.

## Law

Hask's law is a price list. Almost every crime has its fine, posted on boards outside the Watch-house on Gallows
Hill: so many silver for a broken arm, so many for a broken window, more for a broken guild seal. Those who can pay
go home. Those who can't go into the debt-gangs that dredge the Old Cut. A few things cannot be paid off and end on
the gibbets:

- the murder of a guild member;
- forging the city's coin;
- working magic without a licence from the [[Collegium of the Lamp]];
- building, or trying to build, a bridge across the Ambre.

The last is not a joke: the [[Ferryman]] drowns bridge-builders, and the city would rather hang one than lose a ferry
full of citizens.

## Money

Hask mints three coins and accepts everyone else's at a discount. The copper **toe**, stamped with a bare foot, is
the [[Ferryman's Toe|Ferryman's toe]]: one crossing of the river. Ten toes make a silver **oar**, and twenty oars a
gold **barge**. Foreign coin is weighed and clipped by the Moneyers at the money-tables on [[Tollside]], who take a
tenth for the trouble. House Orlemont lends at rates the [[Sunward Church]] calls a sin and everyone else calls
business.

## Neighbours

Hask lives by being useful to everyone and owned by no one.

- **The [[Kingdom of Calder]]** sold Hask its freedom and has regretted it for three hundred years. Calderish barons
  still call the city "the Ford" and talk of buying it back. Wool comes down from the west; Calderish debt goes up.
- **The [[Margravate]]** sends iron and gold down the eastern road and spies in with them. Hask sells it grain,
  rope and silence. The [[Ember Court]] keeps a house on Tollside that everyone pretends is a wool factor's.
- **The [[Sunward Theocracy]]** has a mission in [[Templegate]], the House of the Scale, under the inquisitor
  [[Magdal Crane]]. It preaches against the city's small gods, which is to say against the ferries.
- **The [[Lantern Principalities]]** buy and sell downriver: spice, silk, poison, painted glass. House Velloure
  handles most of it.
- **The [[Thornwold]]** elves allow Hask a fixed number of trees a year. The number is not argued with.
- **[[Deephorn]]**'s dwarves float metal down the Ambre in spring and complain about Hask's tolls in every season.

In the [[Seven Years' War]] Hask sold arrows to all three sides and lent money to two. Sallow intends to do the same
in the next one.

::: dm
**What is really going on.** Sallow keeps the peace by being bought by everyone a little and by no one entirely. The
Margravate pays him most; the Theocracy wants him gone and has a candidate, the Draper guildmaster, who has quietly
taken the sun. The Widow, who runs the [[Quiet Hand]], is Sallow's sister, and her votes in the Council (two
guildmasters owe her their lives or their debts) are why he keeps winning.

**Hooks.**
- *The Warden's election.* Between now and Ferry Night, guildmasters will be bribed, blackmailed and, if nobody is
  careful, drowned. Any side will hire a crew. The players may well end up working for two at once.
- *A bridge.* Someone has hired masons to sink pilings by night upriver of the Ford, at the edge of the
  [[Lady of the Weir]]'s water. The Lady may be behind it; so may a Margravate engineer who wants a road for an army.
- *Buying the Ford back.* A Calderish baron arrives with a chest of gold and a lawyer who has read the charter's
  last clause (see [[Bought Freedom]]). He wants the Ferrymen's copy, the oldest, stolen before anyone else reads it.
:::
