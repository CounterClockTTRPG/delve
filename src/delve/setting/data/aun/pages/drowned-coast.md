At the far south-west of the Reaches the land gives up. West of the [[Sunward Theocracy]]'s white cities the coast
breaks into headlands, sea-caves, mudflats and a scatter of rocky isles, and the sea comes in over what were once
streets. This is the Drowned Coast: smugglers' coves, wreckers' beacons, pirate captains, and the old Thaumarch towns
that sank at the [[Unmaking]] and come up again, dripping, at the lowest tides.

## The sunk towns

When the Thaumarchy fell, the coast fell with it. Whole harbour towns slid under the sea in a night. They are still
there, a few fathoms down, and on the great spring tides, a few times a year, the water draws back far enough to bare
their roofs, their streets and their squares. People wade out with lamps and baskets and take what they can before the
sea returns. The sea returns faster than anyone expects. Every coast family has lost someone to a low tide.

The largest sunk town is Old Calvey, off [[Gullhaven]], where a whole market square shows for an hour at the
spring tides of Marrah and Seremark.

## Who rules

Nobody, and the captains. Each pirate or smuggler captain rules her ship, her cove and whatever village she protects.
When a quarrel grows too big, or the Theocracy's galleys come, the captains meet at the Captains' Table in Gullhaven,
argue for three days, and do what [[Salt Annet]] says. Lesser captains of note include Hobb Laskey of the isle of
Brinescar, who wrecks ships with false lights, and the brothers Quell, who smuggle anything, including people.

## Gods

[[Marrah]] is honoured here more than anywhere, and cursed more. But the coast's own god is the [[Drowned Bell]], a
temple bell sunk somewhere off the isles, which rings before a wreck. Pirates pray to it before a raid, and ships'
boys are taught to listen for it. The [[Sunward Church]] has burned the Bell's shore-shrines twice; they are rebuilt from
driftwood within a month.

## Custom

- **Wreck-right.** What the sea brings ashore belongs to whoever touches it first, unless the Bell rang that night.
  Then a tenth goes back into the sea.
- **No names at sea.** A sailor on the Coast goes by a ship-name and keeps her own for land. Asking a sailor's real
  name is a threat.
- **The Theocracy.** Sunward galleys hunt pirates from [[Candor]]'s ports. A captured pirate is judged at the
  [[Sun's Judgement]] and burned. The coast has a long memory for names of galley-captains.

## For the table

The Coast is where a party sells what it can't sell in [[Hask]], hires a ship with no questions, or goes to vanish.
Prices are high and everyone is armed.

::: dm
**What the coast hides.** The sunk towns are not empty. Their deepest cellars still hold air, and in some of them
something has been living since the Unmaking: pale, patient, and fond of lamplight. Some coast families leave food on
the low-tide stones and do not say for whom.

**The Theocracy's plan.** [[Anselm IX]] means to clear the Coast within five years and has begun buying captains. Hobb
Laskey has already sold.

**Hooks.**
- A spring tide will bare Old Calvey's counting-house for an hour, its vault for ten minutes. A broker in Gullhaven
  sells the party the tide-times. One of them is wrong.
- The Bell rang last night, and no ship was lost. Every captain is waiting to see what it was ringing for.
- The Quell brothers have taken a cargo of Hask children off a barge. [[Mother Gutter]]'s beggars want them back.
