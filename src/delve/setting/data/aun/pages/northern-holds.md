North of the [[Hornwall]], along the cold shore of the Iron Sea, lie the Holds: forty-odd jarldoms in fjords, on
islands and in narrow valleys where the barley ripens late and the winters are six months long. There is no king and
there has never been one. The jarls meet once a year at the [[Hold Moot]] in [[Skarnholm]], settle their feuds, break
some new ones, and decide where to raid.

The Hold-folk are tall, pale and red or flaxen-haired, loud in the hall and quiet on the ice. They are farmers,
fishers, whalers and, in good summers, raiders. Their poets are called skalds, and a jarl without one is a jarl nobody
will remember.

## The land

Black rock, grey sea, white mountains. Each fjord is a world of its own: a hall, a few farms, a boat-shed, a pasture
high up for the summer, and a glacier at the top. The greatest of the glaciers is [[Grandmother Frost]], who keeps the
dead. In winter the fjords freeze and travel is by ski and sledge; in summer it is by ship, and nothing else.

Holds a traveller hears of: **Skarnholm**, the chief port and Moot-place; **Kaldvik**, Skarnholm's rival, a whaling
hold on the outer islands; **Tarnfjord**, where the dwarf-road comes down from the Hornwall; **Brennoy**, the
farthest north, where the sun does not rise for a month of the year.

## Rule and law

A jarl is chosen by the free farmers of his hold, usually from one family, and can be unchosen. Each hold has its own
law-speaker, who knows the law by heart and recites a third of it every year. Wrongs are paid in silver: so much for a
cut hand, so much for an eye, so much for a life. A killing not paid for becomes a feud, and feuds last for
generations.

## Custom

- **Guest-right.** A stranger who asks at the door must be given three nights of food and fire. After three nights he
  is a guest no longer, and had better be useful.
- **The dead.** The dead are carried up to the ice in the spring, sewn in hide, with a knife and a cup. Those who die
  at sea are Marrah's and are not mourned aloud.
- **Gods.** [[Vorm]], the Winter King, is the high god: his priests are few and grim, and keep the calendar of the
  ice. [[Karsk]] gets the raiders' prayers, [[Marrah]] the sailors' curses. Every fjord has its own small god as well,
  in a rock, a waterfall or a whale-bone arch.
- **Raiding.** Raids go out in early summer and come home before the autumn storms. Calder's northern coast, the
  steppe's river mouths and, in bold years, the upper [[Ambre]] are all in reach. A raider brings home silver, cattle
  and slaves; slaves in the Holds go free after seven winters, by law.
- **Prices.** A good longship, 400 gold; a seat on one for the season, a share of the loot; a sword from Deephorn, more
  than a farm.

## Neighbours

The Holds raid [[Kingdom of Calder|Calder]] and the [[Horse Steppe]] and trade with [[Deephorn]] through the
mountain passes. [[Hask]] is a rumour of unimaginable wealth a long way south. The [[Margravate]] has sent envoys to
the Moot twice in the last ten years, with gifts.

## At the table

The Holds are the place for sagas: blood-feuds, sea-crossings, haunted glaciers, a jarl's hall full of drunk heroes
who are mostly murderers. Adventurers can sign on for a raid, carry a body to the ice, or be the strangers whose three
nights are almost up.

::: dm
**The great raid.** [[Sigrun Halfhand]] of Skarnholm wants the whole Moot to raid south together, over the Hornwall
by the orc-held Red Stair, then down the Ambre in river-boats to Hask, something not done since the [[Ash Centuries]]. The Margrave's envoys have promised her
passage and silence. Kaldvik's jarl, Ketil Sornsson, opposes her, mostly because she proposed it.

**Hooks.**
- A Calderish party is shipwrecked in a fjord; the jarl's three nights of guest-right end tomorrow, and a feud is
  about to make them useful.
- Grandmother Frost has given back a body: a jarl dead forty years walks down from the ice and asks for his hall.
- A skald will pay well for a true story of the south to make a saga of. He will make it larger. Its subjects may not
  like that.
