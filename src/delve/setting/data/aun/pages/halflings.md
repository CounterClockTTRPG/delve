The halflings of the Reaches are the **Riverfolk**. They live along the [[Ambre]], from the foot of the [[Hornwall]]
down past [[Hask]] to the Lantern ports, in stilt-houses, mills, boat-yards and riverbank warrens. They are boatmen,
millers, eel-catchers and, to nobody's surprise, smugglers. A great many work Hask's ferries for the
[[Ferrymen's Guild]], and most of the ferry crews are Riverfolk families who have held the same boat for generations.

## What they are like

- **River-wise.** A halfling child can swim before walking and read the Ambre's moods like weather. They know every
  shoal, eddy and backwater for a hundred miles, and will tell you about some of them.
- **Practical.** They like food, fires, a dry bed and a good price. They have no interest in heroics and a great deal
  in salvage.
- **Close.** Riverfolk families are large and know each other's business up and down the river. A message passed
  from boat to boat can travel a hundred miles in two days, without a word written down.
- **Respectful of small gods.** They know the river's [[Small Gods]] better than anyone and deal with them daily. They
  pay the [[Ferryman]] his toe every crossing, leave ribbons for the [[Lady of the Weir]], and do not take sides between
  them, aloud.

## Customs

- A halfling crossing the Ambre spits once over the side for the Ferryman, discreetly. Spitting twice is a bargain;
  three times is an insult.
- Riverfolk weddings are held on two boats lashed together in midstream. If the knot comes loose during the feast, the
  marriage is off, and somebody untied it.
- They call humans "long-legs" and dwarves "sinkers," because dwarves don't swim.
- Saying: "The river takes what you drop," meaning "don't be careless" and also "don't ask where I got this."

## At the table

A halfling character knows the river, its people and its back channels. In Hask they can get a boat at night, find a
cousin on any ferry, and learn what came downriver yesterday. They are underestimated everywhere, which they consider
their best asset. The Sunward Church distrusts them for their small-god dealings; they consider the Sunward Church bad
for trade.

::: dm
The Riverfolk carry most of the smuggled goods through Hask, under the Ferrymen's Guild's nose and with half its
crews' help. The [[Quiet Hand]] takes a cut; the Guild takes a bigger one and calls it a licence fee. Lately the boats
have been carrying crates downriver from the [[Pale Tower]] at night, for someone who pays in Margravate silver.

Hooks:
- A ferry family's boat has been seized by the Guild for unpaid tithe. They want it back before [[Ferry Night]], when
  the Ferryman counts the boats.
- A halfling eel-catcher has netted a brass hand near the weir. The Lady of the Weir wants it back. So does someone in
  the [[Collegium of the Lamp]].
:::
