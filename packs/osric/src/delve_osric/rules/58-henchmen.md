---
title: Recruiting henchmen
source: gmg
section: "2.2.3"
---
Henchmen are classed NPCs of modest level who attach themselves to a higher-level character as companions and supporters, acting in the patron's interest even when apart. The Charisma table limits how many a character may have. Players usually run them, though the DM may rule one refuses a risk.

**How many exist.** Perhaps 1% of a city's people can advance by level, and a tenth of those are willing to serve; the DM may vary this a great deal by place and campaign.

**Methods,** each usable once a month:

- a crier, 10 gp: 1d10% of those available respond;
- posted notices, 50 gp: 10d4%;
- agents, 300 gp: 5d10%, at least 10%;
- buying rounds in taverns: 1d4% per 10 gp spent, at most 5d4%; each establishment visited (up to ten) lowers the others by 1%.

Each extra method used together costs 5% off the total. Applicants turn up over 2d4 days and are lost if the character isn't there to meet them.

**Interviews.** Spells cast on applicants, or blunt questions about alignment, go down badly (detect good/evil or know alignment may be accepted with consent). Reveal only what can be observed; let careful questioning earn hints about alignment, level or health.

**Who comes.** Ancestry follows the local population. Class on d100: fighter 01–40, magic-user 41–60, cleric 61–80, thief 91–00 (81–90 is missing from the transcription; the DM decides). A qualifying fighter is a ranger 1 in 10 and a paladin 1 in 10; a magic-user an illusionist, a cleric a druid, a thief an assassin, each 1 in 6. Demi-humans with two relevant scores over 13 may be multi-classed. Most are 1st level; patrons of 7th–12th draw a 2nd-level one in 10, patrons of 13th and up a 2nd-level one in 4 and a 3rd-level one in 4.
