---
title: Fleeing, parrying, holding and moving
source: pg
section: "1.6.2.3"
---
**Fleeing.** A character in melee who turns and runs gives each opponent who can reach it an immediate free attack at +4, even if that opponent has already attacked this round. After it, the fleer is out of the fight and moves. No free attack is due from an enemy who cannot reach the fleer (for instance because allies stand between them in the front rank), and the fleer may parry it. Moving away when no enemy is within melee range is not fleeing, just movement.

**Parrying.** A character with a weapon in hand who parries makes no attack that round but takes away the Strength and specialisation to-hit bonuses from every melee attack aimed at it. It is only worth doing for someone who has such a bonus. Parrying may be combined with fleeing.

**Holding.** When its segment comes, a character may wait to carry out the declared action until the enemy does something, for instance pulling a trapdoor lever only once the foes are on it, or striking a caster only when the casting starts. It cannot change what was declared, and it must act by segment 6.

**Moving.** A character ignoring the fight to go somewhere moves its full combat movement in its segment. When exact timing matters (can she drink the potion before the charge lands? can the goblins get clear of the blast?), the DM may instead have all declared movement start in segment 1 at a tenth of the round's movement per segment, 12 ft per segment for a rate of 120. A charge may then arrive before its attack is due; that is fine.

Anything not covered by a rule is settled by the DM's common sense.
