---
title: Searching and secret doors
source: pg
section: "1.7.1.8.1"
---
**Quick check of a room:** a turn per 20 × 20 ft. The searchers walk the room, tap floor and walls, and add it to the map. Each searcher gets a chance at the location of any secret door (not how it opens). Open pits are found automatically; each searcher has 2 in 6 to find a closed pit or trapdoor. More searchers make the check more thorough, not faster.

**Secret doors.** Each 10 × 10 ft section searched gives a 1 in 6 chance of finding one that is there; elves and half-elves double it to 2 in 6. Finding how to open it is a second check at the same chance and takes a full turn. The mechanism may not be beside the door, and special doors may hide behind a small puzzle instead of a roll.

The book gives two timings for a secret door search, a round per 10 × 10 ft in one place and a turn in another; treat a thorough search as a turn, and a hurried one as a round with the DM's leave.

**Described searches.** The DM may let careful questions and actions (tapping for a hollow echo, looking for loose stones, odd cracks or a sconce that turns) earn a bonus or even certain success. A description aimed at the wrong place may lower the chance. Such searches take longer than the standard times, and so risk more wandering monsters, which is intended. Agree with the players how much talk versus dice the table wants.
