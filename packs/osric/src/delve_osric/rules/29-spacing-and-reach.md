---
title: Spacing, reach and battle lines
source: pg
section: "1.6.11"
---
**Melee range** is 10 ft for every weapon, fists included: the adjacent squares and the ring beyond them. Two creatures that close are "in melee" whether or not either can actually strike.

- Leaving an enemy's 10-ft range invites its free attack (see fleeing), if it is able to attack.
- Stepping into an enemy's range stops movement: you have closed.
- Spells can be cast in melee but may be disrupted; missiles at someone in melee strike a random participant.

**Basic spacing.** Each small or medium creature fills a 5-ft square; weapon width is ignored. Any weapon reaches an adjacent square. A reach weapon (a spear, say) can strike across a square held by an ally but never across one held by an enemy. So a second rank with reach weapons can hit the enemy's front rank, while the enemy's second rank stays safe from melee.

**Sizes.** Large creatures need 10 × 10 ft to fight, huge 15 × 15 ft, gigantic 20 × 20 ft; the DM rules on squeezing through gaps. Tiny creatures ignore formations and slip in and out of occupied squares, but no more than 8 can attack one small target, 10 a medium one, 12 a large one.

**Battle lines (advanced).** A small or medium fighter actually needs only 3 ft of width. In a passage 9–10 ft wide three can stand abreast, unless weapons need more room: three spears (3 ft each) fit a 10-ft line, three battle axes (4 ft) do not. A 5-ft corridor means single file.

**Second ranks shield the first.** If a front rank holds, the rank behind it can withdraw without giving the enemy free attacks, since the front rank stands between them.
