---
title: Finding and disarming traps
source: pg
section: "1.7.1.8.2"

**Finding.** Dwarfs and gnomes may sense traps in stonework. Thieves and assassins search one object or small area (up to 5 × 5 ft) per attempt, 1–4 rounds each, naming exactly what they check: the door, the chest, the floor before the throne (`skill_check`). Everyone else finds traps by setting them off from a safe distance: a ten-foot pole, a thrown rock, a quick check's tapping.

**Avoiding and disarming.** Many traps can simply be stepped around once found. Disarming is a thief's or assassin's job; others have little chance, though a clever, specific fix (wedging a pressure plate, stopping a gas vent with wax) may work if the DM allows it. Some DMs welcome this kind of solution, others feel it slows play or steals the thief's role, so settle it with the players in advance.

**Triggering.** A trap that is neither found nor avoided goes off half the time when someone passes.

**Damage by depth.** On early levels a trap should rarely do more than 1d6, at worst 1d10, and should not kill outright. Deeper levels, built for strong parties with good saves and many tools, can hold deadlier traps, but even there the DM should usually allow a saving throw or some other way to soften the harm.

Designing traps: see traps and tricks.
