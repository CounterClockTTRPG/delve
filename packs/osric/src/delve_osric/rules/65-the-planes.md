title: The planes
source: gmg
section: "2.5.2"

OSRIC deliberately says little about other planes; they are left open for the DM, and meant for characters of roughly 10th level and up, when the ordinary world is getting easy. The core rules' balance weakens beyond that level anyway.

**The near planes.** The campaign world lies on the Prime Material Plane. Touching it are the astral and ethereal planes, both misty and vague. From them the material world shows only as dim shapes, a human-sized figure visible within about 30 ft. Apart from creatures with special senses, a traveller on those planes cannot be perceived at all from the material world: not seen, heard or otherwise sensed.

- Solid matter on the Prime is no barrier there, so walls can be crossed. Inside a solid object the traveller is blind and must come out into open space; returning to the Prime while inside something is fatal, with no save.
- Arriving on a plane makes a disturbance like a splash in water, which powerful local creatures can sense and come to investigate.
- A character below 10th level can stay only 1d6 turns. After that, something native to the plane devours it: gone for good, beyond any raising short of a wish.

**Further planes** (the hells, the abyss, the elemental and negative planes) are mentioned in places but left vague on purpose. On any other plane the normal rules may not hold: spells, items, to-hit, damage, saves and class powers can all work differently. The DM decides.
