---
title: Loyalty of hirelings and henchmen
source: gmg
section: "2.2.4"
---
Every hireling and henchman has a loyalty score. It starts at 50%, adjusted by the employer's Charisma loyalty modifier, and these guidelines shift it (they also serve for monster troops under a leader, and for morale checks of NPCs in a fight):

- **Employer's alignment:** lawful +10%, good +5%, neutral 0, evil −5%, chaotic −10%.
- **How well the alignments fit:** the same 0; different −10% (allies −5%); opposed −20% (−10%); irreconcilable −30% (−15%).
- **Status:** conscript −20%, hireling −10%, follower 0, henchman +10%.
- **Length of service:** under a month −5%, up to a year 0, then +5% per year to +25% at five years or more.
- **Training:** untrained −30%, semi-trained −20%, trained but untested −10%, trained 0, veteran +10%, elite +20%, leader +30%.
- **Pay:** unpaid −20%, late −15%, very poor −10%, poor −5%, standard 0, good +5%, very good +10%.
- **Treatment:** vicious −20%, cruel −10%, normal 0, kind +10%, generous +20%.
- **Discipline:** brutal −10%, indifferent 0, fair +10%.

While the servant fears the leader and expects to be watched, negative treatment and discipline modifiers count as positive. Heroism, charity, dishonour, being badly outnumbered, or losing a leader can swing loyalty by 50% or more, as the DM judges.

**Testing loyalty.** When tempted (asked to hand over a magic item, a chance to steal unseen, a bribe) or after heavy losses, roll d100; above the adjusted score, the NPC gives in. The score itself describes the NPC: under 01 may desert or worse at any moment; 01–25 disloyal, always out for itself; 26–50 serves but exploits every chance; 51–75 does routine work reliably; 76–100 serves even when it is hard; over 100 fanatical, to the death.

Henchmen should act like people with their own aims, not tools.
