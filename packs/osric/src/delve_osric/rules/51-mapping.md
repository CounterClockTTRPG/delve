---
title: Mapping
source: pg
section: "1.7.1.7"
---
The players' map is an object in the game world, drawn by a character.

- Someone must have light and writing materials, the party must be moving at exploring pace, and measuring takes time (about a turn per 20 × 20 ft).
- If the mapper dies and is left behind, the party is stripped of its gear, or acid or slime eats the parchment, the players lose their map. Backups exist only if someone actually draws them; if the whole party dies below, only copies left on the surface survive.

**How much map.** On small or simple levels none is needed. A trailing map of lines for passages and boxes for rooms usually suffices. Careful maps pay off on tangled levels, where they reveal hidden rooms, loops back to known ground, sloping passages, shifting walls and teleporters.

**The DM's part.** Describe clearly, quickly and in distances, telling only what the characters see. Correct a player map only when the mistake is one the characters would obviously notice, or the description was wrong. A rough sketch can help with an odd-shaped room; do it rarely and never on the players' map. Don't simplify dungeons or draw the map for players who dislike mapping; play goes faster when a player maps, so encourage light, practical mapping.

Maps are worth money to NPCs too: they can be bought, sold, stolen and found in hoards.
