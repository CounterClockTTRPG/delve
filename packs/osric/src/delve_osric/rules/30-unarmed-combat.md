There are three kinds of unarmed combat.

**Brawling** is an ordinary attack roll with fist, foot or pommel, doing 1d2. Everyone is proficient. It suits bar fights and moments without a weapon.

**Grappling** (wrestling, holds) needs both hands free. **Overbearing** (knocking someone down) can be tried with weapon and shield in hand. Both are the tool for taking a foe alive, silencing a sentry or subduing a charmed friend, and for a mob of weak creatures dragging down a strong hero.

**Procedure for grappling and overbearing:**

1. The attacker says which it is attempting.
2. **Fending off.** The defender, and any armed ally next to it (or behind it with a long weapon), may each make a free to-hit roll against the attacker; any hit stops the attempt, dealing no damage. It costs the fender nothing. A defender who is unaware of the attack, or is attacked from behind or the shield-side flank, cannot fend. Each attacker is resolved in turn, so one success after another can hold off a crowd, until one gets through.
3. **Unarmed to-hit.** If not fended off, the attacker rolls d20 against a target number set by its own armour type (2 unarmoured, rising by 2 per AC step to 22 at AC 0 or better); most beasts count as unarmoured. Modifiers: attacker's Dexterity (+1 at 15–18, +2 at 19+) and movement rate (up to +10), defender's armour (up to +7) and movement (up to −10), plus condition modifiers. A natural 1 always fails.
4. **Result.** Roll on the overbearing or grappling result table (see that rule).

Undead and constructs ignore temporary damage. Many-legged creatures cannot be grappled or overborne, though they can overbear others. Unarmed attacks cannot be declared subdual.
