---
title: Running wilderness exploration
source: gmg
section: "2.4.1"
---
Wilderness play goes day by day, ideally on a hex map drawn beforehand. Many planned journeys use a quick travel encounter table to keep pace.

**Weather.** OSRIC gives no weather tables, because the right weather depends so much on the setting; the DM decides it (or uses the campaign's own tables) and tells the party each morning.

**Getting lost.** No chance if the party follows a road, river or other clear feature, uses an accurate map, or someone (a hired guide counts) knows the land. Otherwise it depends on terrain and weather: next to nothing when marching across open grass towards visible mountains, near certain in a foggy forest at night. A fair guideline is 10–25% across normal country with normal care. A lost party wanders off its intended line: usually within a 60° arc ahead, up to 120° on a bad roll, and only in extreme cases completely turned round. The DM rolls secretly and does not tell them.

**Wandering monsters.** Normally two checks by day at 1 in 12 and a third at night (`encounter_check`). Lower the chance in patrolled, settled country and raise it in, say, a forest overrun by goblins. Encounters begin 1d4+4 × 10 yards apart, or 1d3 × 10 yards if either side is surprised, adjusted for ambushes, terrain and sight lines.

**Camping.** Casters must sleep to regain spells, mounts and armoured or weaker characters need rest, and after a forced march rest is essential. No human or demi-human can go four nights without sleep. Sensible parties set watches.

**Foraging** has no rule in the guides; the DM sets a chance by terrain and season.
