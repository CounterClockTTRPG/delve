---
title: Flying and travel by water
source: gmg
section: "2.4.1.5"
---
**Flying.** A flyer climbs at half its movement rate and dives at 45°, losing a foot of height per foot forward; agility class VI ignores both limits. A dive of more than 30 ft gives double damage against targets that are not diving, those on the ground included. Attacking while climbing has no penalty. A ceiling around 5,000 ft (breathable air) is a reasonable limit. Riding a flyer drops its agility one class, except for very large mounts such as dragons.

**Aerial agility** (turning assumes full speed; at half speed a creature turns as one class better):

- **I:** drifts with the wind like a balloon or glides (levitate, flying squirrels).
- **II:** 5 rounds to reach full speed, turns 30° a round (dragons). Must keep at least half speed to stay up.
- **III:** 2 rounds to full speed, 60° a round (sphinx). Half speed minimum.
- **IV:** 1 round to full speed, 90° a round (flying carpet, giant bat). Half speed minimum.
- **V:** full speed or a full stop in 6 segments, 120° a round, can hover (fly spell, mephit).
- **VI:** full speed or stop in 1 segment, hovers, turns 180° (djinni, air elemental).

Larger, heavier flyers generally rank lower, small and light ones higher; there is some play within each class.

**Ships.** Sailing speed depends on the wind. A ship can beat into the wind by tacking, but makes good speed only with the wind somewhere behind. A sea campaign should fix prevailing winds rather than roll them, or ships go nowhere on average.

**Under water,** reached with the right magic: missile weapons and many hand weapons and spells are close to useless. Fire magic fails, and lightning will often strike its caster.
