---
title: Armour Class, descending and ascending
source: pg
section: "1.4.2.4"
---
Armour Class measures how hard a target is to hurt. OSRIC's default is **descending** AC: 10 is unarmoured and lower is better. The **ascending** option counts up instead; Delve writes both, as in "5 [15]", and the two always sum to 20.

**Typical values:** unarmoured 10 [10], leather or padded 8 [12], studded leather or ring 7 [13], scale 6 [14], chain 5 [15], banded or splint 4 [16], plate 3 [17]. A shield improves AC by 1.

**Shields.** A shield only helps against attacks from the front or the shield side, and only against attackers the bearer knows about. A small shield can be used against one attack per round, a medium against two, a large against three; the defender chooses just before each roll. Magic armour and a magic shield both add their bonus.

**To-hit with descending AC.** The attacker reads the class's (or the monster's) to-hit table for the target's AC and needs that number or more on a d20 after modifiers.

**To-hit with ascending AC (formula).** Instead of the table, add the attacker's Base To-Hit Bonus (by class and level, or by Hit Dice for monsters) to the d20; a total equal to or above the target's ascending AC hits. With the formula only, a natural 20 gets an extra +5, which reproduces the table's run of 20s. Do not mix the formula with the tables.

Delve's tools already compute the needed numbers; the DM only has to apply situational modifiers.
