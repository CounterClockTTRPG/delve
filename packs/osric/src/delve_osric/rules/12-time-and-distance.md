**Game time** runs independently of the table: a long fight might take an hour to play and a week's journey a sentence.

- A **segment** is six seconds; it exists only inside combat.
- A **round** is one minute, ten segments. Turns are split into rounds only for combat.
- A **turn** is ten minutes, ten rounds. Dungeon exploration runs turn by turn.
- Six turns make an hour. Outside dungeons and fights the DM counts days or whatever suits.

Mind the order: ten rounds to the turn, never the reverse.

The DM (or a player helping) tracks time because torches burn down, rations are eaten, wounds heal, spells expire, and wandering monster checks fall due. Wasting time should cost the party, usually through more encounters.

**Distance and squares.** OSRIC 3 measures on 5-foot squares. As a standing ruling, a distance starts at the edge of the creature's square, and anything in a square the measured range or radius touches at all is affected: lit by a lantern, splashed by acid, hittable by an arrow. Outdoors, distances given in feet underground usually become yards.

The grid is a convenience, not physics. Two people can share a square (though they fight worse), a character can stand over a fallen friend, and a clever description of reaching over a ledge may earn a few extra feet. The DM sets the squares aside when common sense serves better.
