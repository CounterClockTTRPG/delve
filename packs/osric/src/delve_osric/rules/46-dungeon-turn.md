---
title: The dungeon turn and wandering monsters
source: pg
section: "1.7.1.1"
---
Exploration runs in ten-minute turns. Each turn:

1. **Wandering monster check.** On turns 3, 6, 9 and so on the DM rolls; normally 1 in 6 (`encounter_check`), unless the module's level key says otherwise. On an encounter, skip to step 3.
2. **Actions.** The players say what everyone does; the DM resolves it. Options include: moving (full rate while mapping, five times that through known areas); listening; forcing a door; a quick look into a room; a quick check of a room; searching for traps or secret doors; casting a spell; resting; anything else the DM times and judges.
3. **Encounter.** Roll surprise and distance, judge reactions, and fight if it comes to that (see the combat sequence).
4. **Book-keeping.** Mark the turn, and deduct what was used: hit points, spell durations, torch and lantern time, rations.

Brief actions share a turn; long ones take several.

**A quick look** takes one character with light standing in a doorway for a segment. It reveals open floor, walls and ceiling (lurkers overhead included), writing, and obvious features, but not hidden or fully concealed things. An elf or half-elf gets a 1 in 6 chance to spot a secret door. By infravision alone, only vague shapes are seen, undead may pass for statues, and no writing or secret doors.

**Why wandering monsters matter.** They make the dungeon feel alive without timetables for every resident, and they make dawdling dangerous. Adjust the chance to how the party plays: a quick, quiet party that avoids busy halls should meet fewer.

Deeper levels hold worse monsters and richer treasure (see dungeon encounters), so the players choose their risk.
