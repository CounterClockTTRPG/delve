---
title: Refereeing basics
source: gmg
section: "Introduction"
---
The Gamemaster Guide's central advice for the DM is to be an impartial referee. The DM is not trying to win, and not trying to make the party win; the point is to see what happens when the players' plans, mistakes and dice meet the world. Fairness, more than clever design or acting, is what makes a good DM.

**In practice:**

- **Let consequences stand.** Rescuing the party every time robs a victory of meaning; wanting them to fail is a reason not to run the game.
- **Death should follow from risk the players could see.** OSRIC is deadlier than later games: random monsters are tougher and many threats are save-or-die. If careful players keep dying, give them more warning and explain clearly what killed them.
- **Give information.** Describe threats honestly through the senses: noises, tracks, bones, scorch marks.
- **Read the table.** Some players want to beat the system with the best numbers; others want to follow the story where it leads; some love talk, some tactics. Balance them; the group should broadly agree on how heroic or villainous the party is.
- **Start small.** A published adventure and a village near a ruin are enough. Nobody knows every rule; look up what you can quickly, otherwise make a ruling and keep playing.
- **Play fast.** Roll dice in handfuls, use morale to end fights that are decided, skip dull stretches, and share book-keeping with the players.
- **The world, not a plot.** The DM builds a world; the players' characters want things and go after them. Story is what comes out of play.

When the rules are silent, the DM decides, and says it is a ruling.
