---
title: Resting and other dungeon actions
source: pg
section: "1.7.1.9"
---
**Rest periods.** A party exploring must rest one turn in every six, one turn after each fight, and two turns after a chase or escape. While resting nobody moves or does anything strenuous, though quiet work such as mapping is fine. Pick the spot with care: a small side room with one door that can be spiked is good; an open corridor or the head of a stair is a poor place even for a short rest and dangerous for a long one. A party that stays below for hours may hole up for a whole night to regain spells.

**Skipping rest** leaves everyone fatigued. The DM sets the effect (slower movement, combat penalties, lowered scores, worse NPC morale), growing worse the longer the party goes on.

**Other actions.** Class and ancestry powers such as a dwarf judging depth, a gnome finding direction, a paladin sensing evil or a ranger tracking take a round per try unless stated.

Beyond those, OSRIC has no skill system on purpose: heroes ride, swim and climb ordinary walls without rolling. For anything really hard, the DM sets a chance from the character's class, level, ancestry and scores, perhaps borrowing a known roll (the Strength chance for doors and bars, the Intelligence chance to learn spells, Constitution's system shock, or a fitting saving throw). Usually the DM states the odds and lets the player roll (`ability_check`); the DM may roll in secret when the situation calls for it.
