---
title: Random wilderness encounters
source: gmg
section: "2.9"
---
**Which table.** Use the terrain that dominates the party's current hex: foothills that are mostly hills with a few peaks use the hills table. Columns: aquatic (under water), marine (on ships or flying over sea), arctic, tundra, desert, forest, jungle, hills (foothills too), mountains, plains, rural (settled country), wetlands (swamp and marsh), graveyard (ruins, old battlefields and tombs, heavy on undead) and a pulp "lost world".

**Procedure.** Roll on the main table's terrain column for a category (airborne, animal, humanoid, water, NPC and so on), then on that terrain's sub-table for the creature. A "water" result is a creature from a nearby river, lake or sea coming out to hunt; if no water is near, reroll the category. `encounter_check` does the rolling.

**Numbers.** Wilderness encounters are not scaled to the party. Use each monster's "number encountered" from its entry (`monster_lookup`), and its lair chance to see whether the party has stumbled on its home, where its treasure is. The wilderness is dangerous by design; players should learn to run.

**NPC encounters** come in kinds that suggest a situation:

- **Decoys** lure the party into danger or distract it.
- **Escapees** have fled captivity, with little gear and pursuers close behind.
- **Joiners** want to join the party, for good or ill.
- **Runners** flee or avoid something, properly equipped.
- **Searchers** look for an item or a person.
- **Survivors:** one or two shaken leftovers of a party that was wiped out, usually hurt.
- **Wolves in sheep's clothing:** creatures passing as human (lycanthropes, doppelgangers, polymorphed beings), not necessarily hostile.

The lost world tables mix in time travellers, cavemen and stranger things; the DM may extend them freely. For regions the DM has detailed, a custom table matched to the area's difficulty is better than the generic ones.
