---
title: Hirelings
source: gmg
section: "2.2.1"
---
NPCs who serve the party come in kinds with different terms: hirelings, henchmen, followers, and temporary associates.

**Zero-level folk.** An untrained human has 1d4 hp, a trained one such as a soldier 1d6; half-orcs and half-elves 1d6 either way; other demi-humans as their monster entries.

**Hirelings** are unclassed people who work for pay and do not advance by level. The employer equips them fully. Torchbearers, porters and soldiers will not go below the third dungeon level, and for dungeon work want triple their daily wage plus a quarter share of treasure.

**Shares.** Count four shares per character, two per henchman, one per hireling; divide treasure (and XP) by the total. So a hireling takes a quarter share and a henchman a half.

**Standard hirelings** (labourers, cooks, grooms, porters, servants, teamsters, carpenters, masons and so on) are easy to find for a few days, paid by the day or month (labourers 1 sp a day, masons 4 sp, teamsters 5 sp, limners 10 sp). Monthly rates assume lodging and keep are also provided. For a long engagement only one in six agree, rising to one in two if two or three days' extra pay is offered; the DM may use the reaction roll instead. A porter carries 50 lb, twice that with a pole. Materials cost extra, about a tenth of the finished price.

**Expert hirelings** (alchemists, armourers, smiths, engineers, gemcutters, sages, scribes, spies, stewards, weaponers, ship crews, mercenary soldiers) sign on for a month or more and mostly serve characters with a stronghold. Their monthly cost covers wages and keep but not soldiers' arms and armour. Towns usually have at least one free mercenary unit, cities 1d4+2; groups of six to ten have a sergeant, bigger ones officers.
