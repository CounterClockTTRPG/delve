A saving throw is a d20 roll to escape or lessen a spell, trap, poison or special attack. Meeting or beating the target number succeeds. The target number depends on class and level (`class_lookup`; `saving_throw` rolls it) and on which of five categories the threat falls in:

1. **Aimed magic items:** rods, staves and wands.
2. **Breath weapons.**
3. **Death, paralysis and poison.**
4. **Petrifaction and polymorph.**
5. **Spells:** any spell, and anything not covered by the other four.

A success either cancels the effect (most poisons) or reduces it (a fireball does half). A natural 1 always fails; whether a natural 20 always succeeds is the table's choice. Which category applies when two seem to fit is the DM's call; the more specific one usually wins over "spells".

**Common adjustments:**

- Wisdom adjusts mental saves (charm, fear and the like, as the DM rules).
- Dexterity's agility modifier applies to saves against aimed magic items and area spells, and wherever dodging plainly matters.
- Dwarfs and gnomes add a "stalwart" bonus by Constitution against aimed magic items, poison and spells; halflings get one against poison and spells.
- 25% cover or more: +2 where agility helps. Invisible: +4. Blinded: −4.
- Poisons may carry their own modifier, from +2 for weak venom to −4 for deadly toxins.
- Magic items such as rings and cloaks of protection add their bonus.

Multi-classed characters use whichever class table is better for that save. Monsters save by Hit Dice on their own tables (see monster attacks and saves). A character who fails a save may also see carried gear tested (item saving throws).

At the DM's option, a skill-like task can borrow the save that fits it: clerics shine at death saves, magic-users at spell saves.
