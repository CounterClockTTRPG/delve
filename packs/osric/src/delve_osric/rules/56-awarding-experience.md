---
title: Awarding experience
source: gmg
section: "2.1.4"
---
The DM awards XP for treasure, for defeating monsters, and for missions.

- **Treasure:** the party earns one XP for each gold piece's worth of coins, gems and jewellery brought safely to a base or town. Gems are counted at their value when found, not after a gemcutter improves them.
- **Magic items:** a kept item earns a tenth of its gold value (half for potions); sold (to anyone but a henchman of the party), the full value.
- **Missions:** use sparingly, when an adventure is worth more than its loot (driving off wolves that own nothing for poor villagers).
- **Monsters:** for defeating them, which need not mean killing. Each monster entry gives its value as a base plus an amount per hit point ("135 +4/hp"); `xp_earned` totals it at the end of a fight.

**Valuing a new monster or NPC.** Start from the base value for its Hit Dice. For each special ability add the "special" amount for that row; for each exceptional ability, the "exceptional" amount; then add the per-hit-point amount for each hit point the individual has. A 3 HD monster with one special and one exceptional ability is worth 50 + 15 + 60 = 125, plus 2 per hp.

- **Special** abilities: casting spells as a 1st–3rd level caster, one spell-like power of level 1–3, immunity to normal weapons, three or more attacks, and the like.
- **Exceptional** abilities: a dragon's breath, casting as a 4th-level-plus caster, a power above 4th spell level, very low AC, very high damage, petrifying gaze and similar.

It is an art more than a science. Hirelings and henchmen take their share of XP as of treasure (see hirelings). Undead destroyed by turning give none.
