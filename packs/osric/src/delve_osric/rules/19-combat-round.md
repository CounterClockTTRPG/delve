---
title: The combat sequence
source: pg
section: "1.6.1"
---
When a fight starts, time drops from turns to one-minute rounds of ten six-second segments.

**Encounter distance.** Normally 1d4+4 × 10 ft (yards outdoors). If either side is surprised it is 1d3 × 10 ft (or yards). The DM may change it for ambushes, terrain and sight lines.

**Each fight runs:**

1. Roll surprise (d6 per side) and the distance; play out any surprise segments.
2. Players declare actions and spells; the DM settles what the monsters will do.
3. Each side rolls initiative (d6, low acts first).
4. The side with the lower roll acts in its segment and results take effect, except spells, which go off when their casting time ends. Some actions let the other side cut in: an attack on someone fleeing, or a weapon set against a charge.
5. The other side acts in its segment.
6. The round ends; if the fight goes on, return to step 2.

**Declaring actions.** Before initiative each player says what the character will do: move, take a combat action (attack, charge, close, flee, parry, set against a charge, fire missiles, negotiate), cast a named spell, or do something else (drink a potion, climb, pull a lever when the enemy steps on the trap). A contingency is allowed ("parry if they charge, otherwise flee").

Once initiative is rolled, plans are fixed. If a plan becomes impossible, the DM usually allows the nearest sensible action, but a declared spell cannot be swapped: the caster casts it or does nothing. Declaring first is deliberate; it rewards winning initiative. With per-character initiative, at least spells should still be declared in advance.
