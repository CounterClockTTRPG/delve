---
title: Random dungeon encounters
source: gmg
section: "2.7.3.1"
---
When a wandering monster check comes up, a module's own table takes precedence. Otherwise use OSRIC's tables (the check itself is `encounter_check`):

1. Roll d20 against the dungeon level on the matrix to find the **monster level** (1–10) of the encounter. Deeper dungeon levels shift the odds towards higher monster levels.
2. Roll d100 on that monster level's table for the creature.
3. Use the **Wandering** column for how many appear on a check, or the **Lair** column when stocking a room.

**Adjusting numbers to depth.**

- A monster from a lower monster level met on a deeper dungeon level comes in larger numbers: multiply its count by the number of levels it sits below the dungeon's level (goblins at 1d6 on level 1 become 3d6 on dungeon level 3).
- A monster from a higher monster level met nearer the surface comes in fewer: subtract one per level of difference (1d8 shadows on monster level 4 become 1d8−1 on dungeon level 3), with fewer or no attendants.
- NPC parties met deeper go up in character level rather than number; 9th and 10th level monsters met deeper bring minions instead of more of themselves.

**NPC parties.** Always 2–5 characters (1d4+1), filled out with henchmen and hirelings to nine in all. Roll each character's class on the NPC class table; the DM drops or changes impossible combinations (an assassin travelling with a paladin) and results beyond a class's maximum.

Wilderness lairs work differently (see monster statistics); in dungeons, lairs are keyed rooms.

Random results need judgement: a weak monster with a huge hoard, or an encounter that makes no sense where it falls, can be changed or ignored.
