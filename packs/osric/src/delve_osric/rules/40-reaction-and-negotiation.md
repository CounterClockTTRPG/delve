---
title: Reaction and negotiation
source: pg
section: "1.6.2.8"
---
Talking can avert or delay a fight, through honesty or bluff. The DM usually just decides how a creature reacts, but can roll d100 (`reaction`), adding the speaker's Charisma reaction adjustment; let the most charismatic character do the talking.

| d100 | Reaction |
|---|---|
| 01–05 | Very hostile: monsters attack; in society, flat refusal, being thrown out, or prompt steps to harm the speaker. |
| 06–25 | Hostile: monsters probably attack unless clearly outmatched; socially, revenge may wait for a better moment. |
| 26–45 | Unfavourable: monsters probably attack; the next actions lean against the speaker, 55% of the time. |
| 46–55 | Neutral: monsters hold off unless sure to win; NPCs want to hear more. |
| 56–75 | Favourable: monsters wait for an offer; the next actions lean towards the speaker, 55% of the time. |
| 76–95 | Friendly: likely to help if there is something in it for them. |
| 96–00 | Very friendly: may help even at a loss. |

The same roll serves for hirelings weighing a job, merchants haggling and courtiers judging a petition. For small matters, the DM may simply set a chance (say 50% plus the Charisma adjustment) and roll once.

Negotiating is a combat action like any other: it is declared before initiative and happens in the speaker's segment. Monsters that are hungry, guarding a lair, or of a kind that hates the speakers should get a worse result than the dice alone suggest; the DM adjusts for alignment, language and what each side wants.
