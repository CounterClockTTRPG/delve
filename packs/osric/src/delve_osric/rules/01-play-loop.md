---
title: How play runs
source: pg
section: "1.5"
---
Play is a conversation that repeats: the DM sets the scene, the players ask what their characters notice, the DM answers, the players say what their characters do, and the DM tells them what comes of it and what they now see. Combat is the same loop made stricter, with a fixed order inside each round.

The DM describes only what the characters can perceive: sights, sounds, smells, the feel of a place. NPCs are voiced by the DM, in character or summarised.

**Natural rolls.** A natural roll is the face of the die before any modifier.

- On a saving throw a natural 1 always fails. Whether a natural 20 always succeeds is a table choice.
- On an attack roll the book makes neither 1 nor 20 automatic; a natural 1 is not a certain miss and a natural 20 not a certain hit. Many tables house-rule automatic misses and hits on them, which helps hordes of weak foes against well-armoured heroes.
- Fumbles (a dropped weapon on a 1) and critical hits (maximum damage on a 20) are house rules, not OSRIC; they tend to favour the monsters.

When no rule covers a situation, the DM rules from common sense and says that it is a ruling.
