title: Towns and cities
source: gmg
section: "2.5.1"

**Offstage by default.** Most town business (gathering rumours, hiring guides and soldiers, shopping, selling treasure, healing, paying casters to identify items or lift curses, training for levels) can happen between scenes: the players adjust their sheets and gold, the DM notes the days that pass, and play resumes at the next expedition.

**Played out.** Sometimes the group wants a scene in town: interviewing hirelings, bargaining with a wizard to undo a curse on a friend, or a whole session of intelligence-gathering. Some adventures live mostly in town (a murder to solve, intrigue at court). These run looser than dungeons: no maps, little tracking of torches and food, no marching order, and the party often splits up.

**When town turns dangerous** (a night in the thieves' quarter), go back to dungeon procedure: ten-minute turns, movement in tens of feet (usually the fast rate of five times normal when not mapping or wary of traps), and an encounter check every third turn. Street encounters are mostly ordinary people: beggars, urchins, pickpockets, the watch. A city encounter table keyed to districts is best; the DM may ignore unlikely results and should describe NPCs vaguely enough that the players cannot tell who matters.

**Pitfalls.** Town play is one-to-one talk between a player and the DM, so others sit idle; it lacks a clear sequence, so things get missed. It suits experienced DMs, small groups and players who enjoy acting in character. Bored players may start fights for something to do; that is often as much the DM's fault as theirs.

Hirelings, sages, temples and markets are found here (see hirelings and henchmen).
